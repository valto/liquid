# Capture for Unknown Future Value

**Definition.** Preserve appropriate source richness before anticipated questions determine the full extent of what remains.

A selected field or summary can be useful while failing to preserve evidence needed for an unforeseen question. Appropriate originals retain possibilities for later interpretation and reprocessing.

Text, documents, images, audio, spatial observations and sensor information offer different evidence. No sensor combination captures all reality. Preservation is governed by purpose, consent, rights, retention and practical resources.

## Evidence and interpretation

Keep an original observation distinct from an inferred assertion and a derived index. Structured facts can themselves be authoritative when they originate as structured records. SQL is useful; the concern is losing evidence by retaining only anticipated fields.

## Separate preservation from processing investment

Retaining an appropriate original can preserve future options without immediately generating every summary, embedding, index or graph. Select processing and caching around actual demand and commitments, including sufficient metadata and security to make discovery practical.

Count preservation costs, rights, retention and future reprocessing alongside useful retrieval value. Retiring a derived optimization does not confer permission to delete its source. This distinction supports selective investment; it is not a claim that all information should be collected or that all upfront processing is unnecessary.

## Test future usefulness

Ask new questions against retained evidence, record what can and cannot be concluded, and compare the value and cost of preservation. Visible bird behavior, for example, may support an observation without establishing actual health.
