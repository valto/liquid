# Parallel Realizations

**Definition.** Maintain and evaluate compatible realizations of a capability separately from decisions about their release, adoption and retirement.

## Abstract

A new version can exist without replacing every existing experience. This concept paper connects stable capability meaning with parallel implementations, contextual interfaces and deliberate retirement. Established software delivery practices provide concrete precedents. Extending the pattern to operating processes and business models is the supplied companion's broader proposal, subject to compatibility, authority and support economics.

## Separate the decisions

Construction produces a candidate. Validation examines whether it meets the relevant contract. Release makes an approved alternative available within a defined scope. Adoption determines who actually uses it. Retirement ends support under explicit conditions. Combining these into one upgrade event can hide distinct costs and choices.

More than one active realization can be legitimate. An expert workspace and a focused workspace might share capability semantics while serving different tasks. A deterministic path might handle validated cases while an intelligent path remains available for exceptions. The alternatives need not be positions on a universal progression.

## A version needs an intelligible record

Retain the capability and its CRD or equivalent requirements, the realization's contracts, supported contexts, dependencies, security and validation evidence, operating costs, compatibility, owner, status and retirement conditions. This is proposed logical metadata rather than a compulsory storage format.

Experimental, candidate, active, cohort-active, supported legacy and retired statuses express different conditions. A status label does not establish authority or reliability by itself. Readers and users should be able to determine which version is applicable to their task and what commitments remain.

## Compatibility includes consequences

Two paths can return similarly shaped data while disagreeing about meaning, identity or permission. Compare accepted outcomes, formats, state transitions, entitlements and obligations. A stable interface can sit above evolving implementations; a new interface can use an unchanged backend. Both arrangements need the applicable contract preserved.

Shadow evaluation should avoid duplicate side effects. Running two interpretations over authorized evidence can support comparison; making two purchases or sending two messages is a different consequence. A parallel design needs explicit ownership of real effects, state reconciliation and recovery where relevant.

## Illustrative situation

A recurring supplier comparison is performed through an agent. A validated rules-and-calculation implementation is developed for its formalizable portion. The agent remains active while the candidate is evaluated on authorized representative cases, including exceptions and changed policies.

The candidate can become active for covered cases while interpretation remains agentic. Adopting the exact path does not change who can commit a purchase. Retirement of an older path depends on coverage, support costs, dependencies and the required fallback. A candidate's successful demonstration alone is insufficient.

## Precedents and the proposed extension

[Danilo Sato's Parallel Change](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/ParallelChange.html) describes expand, migrate and contract phases for interface evolution. [Pete Hodgson's Feature Toggles](https://martinfowler.com/articles/feature-toggles.html) distinguishes flag purposes and their testing and carrying costs. [Google Cloud's canary documentation](https://docs.cloud.google.com/deploy/docs/deployment-strategies/canary) describes staged traffic between existing and new software versions.

These primary sources establish specific delivery patterns. They do not prove that every workflow or business model can coexist safely, or that all users must always have a choice. Security, safety, contractual or regulatory obligations can require a transition. The appropriate decision follows the actual context.

## Evaluate the portfolio

Measure sustained beneficiary value alongside the compatibility matrix, duplicated upkeep, security exposure, support load and legacy dependencies. Parallel versions can create flexibility and can become an expensive permanent burden. Record when continued diversity is valuable and what would justify consolidation.

Use the current accepted baseline and feasible alternatives. Account for cohort self-selection and differing tasks. Newest is not a success criterion; retaining an existing realization can be the evidence-supported result.
