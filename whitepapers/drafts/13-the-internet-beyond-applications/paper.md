# The Internet Beyond Applications

*Activities, information flows and economic relationships beneath the software layer*


**Conceptual source and direction: Valto Loikkanen.** Research and drafting: Codex (OpenAI), at his request. Research draft 1.0 · 26 November 2026.

## Abstract

Valto Loikkanen's Internet on One Page model describes activities, actors, information flows and revenue arrangements without making a particular application the organizing center. This paper uses the supplied diagram, the eleven-page November 2026 conceptual whitepaper and the shared discussion to examine how adaptive interaction and Liquid Software could serve those underlying relationships. It distinguishes a conceptual economic map from legal classifications and market predictions. The principal research question is whether adaptable implementation can increase effective choice and value creation while information ownership, human authority and market incentives remain explicit. [S01, S02]

## 1. What the diagram makes visible

The Internet on One Page identifies seven activities: create, control, promote, connect, discover, acquire and consume. It shows humans, AI agents and organizations exchanging information, with several information categories and economic arrangements beneath the activity sequence.

Applications are not the fundamental categories of the map. A particular application may support several activities, while the same activity may be performed through several tools or participants. This makes the model useful for considering a digital economy in which interfaces and implementations change more readily.

The supplied whitepaper distinguishes direct human participation from delegated agent operation across the same infrastructure. Its two interconnected internets are functional perspectives, rather than separate physical networks or an obligatory replacement of human experiences. It also separates information owners, objective setters, decision makers, operational actors and beneficiaries. These distinctions explain why changing an interface need not change who controls the relationship. [S02, pp. 4, 7–9]

## 2. Activities as durable capability categories

| Activity | Underlying concern | Adaptive possibility |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Create | Produce information, knowledge or an offering | Compose appropriate tools around the current need |
| Control | Govern information, use and authority | Preserve decisions across changing implementations |
| Promote | Communicate availability and value | Adapt communication to the audience and context |
| Connect | Establish relationships and interaction | Choose a mutually suitable language and channel |
| Discover | Find relevant information and capabilities | Combine intelligent discovery with precise access |
| Acquire | Obtain an offering, right or access | Support an agreed transaction through different forms |
| Consume | Use information or capability for a purpose | Present and execute in a contextually useful way |

These are broad categories, not seven mandatory software modules or complete CRDs. A particular capability should still be defined at a meaningful operational scope.

## 3. Information categories and their limits

The diagram associates promotional information with attention value, public-domain information with free value, owned information with monetary value, and private or personal information with personal and targeting value. [S02]

These labels are a conceptual map of economic roles, not mutually exclusive legal categories. Owned information can be freely shared; personal information can have commercial relevance; and publicly accessible material is not necessarily public domain. The proposed architecture must keep actual rights, purpose and permission explicit rather than infer them from a diagram label.

Adaptive presentation does not alter the underlying permission. An agent that can summarize valuable information is not automatically entitled to acquire, disclose or monetize it.

## 4. Revenue models can outlast interfaces

| Arrangement in the diagram | Relationship | What remains important as software changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Licensing | Licensor and licensee | Scope, permitted use and attribution |
| Advertising | Advertiser and channel | Incentives, disclosure and attention allocation |
| Transaction fees | Broker and parties | Service responsibility and transaction outcome |
| Commerce | Seller and buyer | Offering, payment and accepted commitment |
| Subscriptions | Provider and subscriber | Ongoing entitlement and continuity |

An adaptive system can support these relationships through a website, message exchange, application or agent interaction. The economic arrangement still requires understandable terms and accepted effects.

The disappearance of a dedicated marketplace screen would not eliminate market power, liability or bargaining. These concerns can move into discovery, identity, data access, payment or agent selection.

## 5. An illustrative exchange

A person wants an analysis using a specialist dataset. Their representative discovers an available offering, identifies applicable terms, obtains permitted access and delivers an appropriate result. The seller may fulfill the need through an existing query or intelligent assembly.

The interaction could use a human-readable channel, a structured request or a negotiated combination. A dedicated application is optional in the example; the parties, rights, meaning and accepted transaction are not.

This is a proposed scenario. It does not establish legal enforceability, market adoption or technical interoperability among current systems.

## 6. Independence, representation and incentives

An agent can act for a principal while also depending on a provider whose incentives differ. The principal should be able to understand who operates the agent, what interests it serves and when it receives compensation or preferential information.

The conversation mentions a Personal Intelligence Fiduciary as a complementary idea. Here it is a design question about loyal representation and conflicts of interest, not an assertion that an AI service has a legally established fiduciary status. The supplied whitepaper similarly identifies interest alignment as a requirement and distinguishes an agent's operational ability from its authority to act. [S01, S02, pp. 9–10]

Cheaper customization does not itself protect the customer's interests. A system can generate a personalized interface that strongly favors its operator. Effective agency requires information continuity, clear delegation, relevant transparency and a practical way to replace the representative.

## 7. Open interaction and concentration

PAIX explores an open entry and fallback channel with explicit optional upgrades. Solid and local-first work explore ways to separate user information and control from applications. These are relevant design comparisons, not evidence that an independent market will automatically emerge. [S10, S29, S05]

Liquid Software could reduce some barriers to specialized service creation. It could also increase dependence on a few providers of intelligence, trusted context or distribution. The key empirical issue is where control and switching costs move.

An open interface is useful only if participants can actually use it under workable terms, preserve their information and retain responsibility for accepted commitments.

The supplied whitepaper proposes evaluating intermediaries by their distinct contributions: navigation, information aggregation, trust, transaction coordination and market access. Agents may alter the value of navigation while leaving verification or physical delivery valuable. A useful comparison therefore follows the service each intermediary performs and who benefits, rather than treating an entire platform category as destined to disappear. These remain analytical propositions; the supplied document explicitly reports no external research or empirical validation. [S02, pp. 9–11]

## 8. Research questions

Evaluate a bounded exchange across several presentation and implementation choices. Can the parties discover the offering, understand terms, determine authority, complete the transaction and preserve the result? What happens when a participant changes its agent or software provider?

Measure total transaction cost, comprehension, failures, switching effort, supplier choice and distribution of benefit. Include situations where conventional platforms provide superior trust or coordination.

Longer-term claims require observing real market behavior. Faster interface creation alone does not establish increased competition or reduced concentration.

## 9. Contribution

The Internet on One Page provides an economic and information-flow context for the technical papers. Its activities and relationships can remain recognizable while the software serving them changes.

The contribution of the combined argument is to ask which parts of the economy are fundamental commitments among principals and which are contingent implementation arrangements. That distinction can guide research without assuming that software flexibility dissolves the institutions needed for exchange.
