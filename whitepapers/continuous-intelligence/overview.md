# Continuous Intelligence, Adaptive Evolution, and Stable Experience

*Continuous intelligence. Adaptive evolution. Stable operation and experience.*

Conceptual source: **Valto Loikkanen**. The supplied companion whitepaper is **version 1.0, dated 28 November 2026**, with 18 physical PDF pages, 13 numbered chapters and three diagrams. The separately supplied source package contains the complete Markdown, three SVG originals and a README. This overview and the related concept papers are additional AI-assisted synthesis and research.

The paper adds a temporal dimension to Liquid Software: what should be checked, why, how deeply and when? If the evidence supports change, how can a system introduce it while preserving what already works? Faster sensing need not mean continuous changes to production or to a person's familiar workspace.

## Six independently useful concept papers

- [Layered Intelligence Cycles](https://valto.github.io/liquid/concepts/layered-intelligence-cycles/): review purpose, depth, cadence and triggers.
- [Meta-Learning](https://valto.github.io/liquid/concepts/meta-learning/): evaluate how the system notices, reviews and learns.
- [Stability-Aware Evolution](https://valto.github.io/liquid/concepts/stability-aware-evolution/): compare future states together with their transition paths.
- [Parallel Realizations](https://valto.github.io/liquid/concepts/parallel-realizations/): separate release, adoption and retirement.
- [Reasoning Compilation](https://valto.github.io/liquid/concepts/reasoning-compilation/): materialize formally checkable portions with explicit assumptions and invalidation.
- [Outcome Verification](https://valto.github.io/liquid/concepts/outcome-verification/): determine whether the beneficiary's original problem improved.

These are complementary proposals. Using one does not require deploying the entire architecture or any named vendor product. The supplied paper also deepens the existing Adaptive Interface and Adaptive Information Retrieval concepts.

## Evidence and interpretation

Pendo's 2019 research describes aggregate usage across 615 subscriptions and three months. Low feature use does not prove low value or justify removing the same features for every user. The Optivian example reports 933,294 versus 158,691 billable tokens for a representative customer run, approximately 5.88 times the latter count, and estimated per-run costs of $2.96 versus $0.53. These are vendor-reported figures; the source does not establish an independently matched full-lifecycle comparison.

MDIL's public overview, review summary and connection page describe declared reasoning and reusable checked work. Their access wording differs: some material describes a 17 November beta launch while the connection page says its public alpha sandbox opens late in 2026. This review establishes what the vendor says, rather than verified access, universal correctness, CRD conformance or independent replication. The full system card was not read in this intake.

Progressive disclosure, customization, feature flags, parallel change and canary rollout supply related established practices. Extending them to the whole proposed organizational architecture remains a hypothesis. The public customization-report overview was read; its full 93-page report was not.

The original bibliography attributes two articles to their host, Martin Fowler. The articles themselves identify **Danilo Sato** as author of *Parallel Change* and **Pete Hodgson** as author of *Feature Toggles*. The original bibliography remains unchanged; the [research index](https://valto.github.io/liquid/research/) records the more precise attribution.

The source's note that the Liquid Software home could not be retrieved concerns its own preparation. The current canonical repository was available for this intake. Its reference to a 28 November discussion does not mean that separate discussion was supplied or archived here; the previously preserved discussion snapshot remains its own source.
