Principles

Durable meaning and human authority allow implementations and representations to change coherently.

Conceptual source: Valto Loikkanen · AI-assisted synthesis and research

Read source text ↗

Preserve attributable information

Retain appropriate source evidence, accepted revisions and provenance. Distinguish an original, an interpretation and a derived retrieval structure. Preservation remains governed by purpose, rights and resources.

Keep meaningful human authority

Make delegation, prior decisions and reserved choices understandable. Technical availability is not permission. Operational autonomy stays within the principal's actual authority and applicable commitments.

Let context determine interaction

What is needed, by whom, where and why guide how information is presented and exchanged. Languages and formats carry needs and information; shared meaning guides interpretation.

Separate capability from realization

A capability's purpose and accepted outcome can persist through different implementations. Changing or retiring code does not silently change a commitment or erase the information it serves.

Prefer useful adaptation

Create, retain or retire software using observed need and measured benefit. Stable implementations remain legitimate choices. Include quality, reliability, cost, recovery and uncertainty in the comparison.

Keep proposals testable and attributable

Distinguish established technology, related prior work, the conceptual synthesis and experiments. Preserve corrections and source versions. Agreement between AI-assisted summaries is not independent empirical corroboration.

Compare cumulative value over time

Choose feasible realizations by accepted outcomes and cumulative net value, including time to value, full lifecycle costs, risk, transitions and retained flexibility. Cost minimization assumes comparable benefits and timing. Avoid counting the same opportunity or failure loss twice.

Invest selectively in processing

Preserve appropriate sources and choose processing, caching and preprocessing around demand, freshness and commitments. Include necessary discovery and security work. Retiring a derived structure does not change source authority or authorize source deletion.

These economic and processing principles are developed from The Economics of Execution, version 2.0.

Separate learning from forced change

Sensing, understanding, reviewing, releasing and adopting can proceed at different rates. Define review purpose, depth, cadence and triggers. Evaluate the learning process itself, including missed signals and unnecessary disruption.

Give stable experience value

Compare future outcomes together with transitions, familiarity, switching and support costs. Retain an accepted realization or offer a parallel alternative when justified. Contextual modes and personal compositions need not converge on one expert interface.

Compile only checkable portions

Validate formally expressible logic independently, retain its assumptions and define invalidation. Deterministic execution is insufficient evidence of truth, completeness or freshness.

Verify the original need

Connect signals, decisions and execution to beneficiary outcomes. Consider internal throughput and external fitness. Completed tasks, feature adoption and token savings measure parts of the system; they do not alone establish sustained improvement.

These additional principles are developed from the Continuous Intelligence companion, a conceptual source with explicit research and vendor-evidence limits.
