Liquid Software for governance and policy

Examine represented principals, delegated freedom, accountability and obligations that must survive adaptation.

Conceptual source: Valto Loikkanen · AI-assisted synthesis and research

Read source text ↗Give this to your agent

Liquid Software proposes that implementations can adapt while human decisions, rights and accepted commitments remain intelligible. For governance, policy and risk readers, the main question is how can adaptation preserve accountability and meaningful control?

The resource's human-sovereignty framing depends on actual authority, traceable evidence and enforceable boundaries. A useful description of those boundaries is only part of the work.

Separate the people and roles in an interaction

The immediate requester, represented principal, beneficiary and information owner can differ. An agent preparing a briefing may act for an organization while handling records governed by other people or agreements. Choosing an interface or execution path does not resolve those relationships by itself.

The reference architecture keeps principal, information, capability, interaction and governance concerns visible. A capability describes an accepted ability; authority establishes what a particular actor may do in a particular context.

Make delegated freedom understandable

Degrees of Decision Freedom concerns the space created by prior decisions. A principal can delegate choice of retrieval method while reserving spending or disclosure decisions. The same actor may have different freedoms for different operations.

Meaningful control therefore involves scope, usable guidance, revision, escalation, intervention and revocation. It need not mean separate approval for every already-authorized step. Equally, permission to analyze or propose does not automatically include committing funds, deploying a change or withdrawing an accepted service.

Distinguish review, release and adoption

The Continuous Intelligence companion separates sensing, reviewing, deciding, releasing, adopting and retiring. A trigger can initiate an examination without granting production authority. A released alternative may remain optional or serve a bounded cohort; adoption and withdrawal have their own consequences.

Parallel realizations must preserve obligations and enforceable access boundaries. User control over a familiar experience is valuable where the context permits it; urgent security or other binding obligations can require change. Inferred preferences do not authorize forced rearrangement or disclosure. Organizational capabilities do not automatically gain access to personal Core information.

Keep flexibility and obligations separate

Incidental workflow restrictions may become unnecessary as better implementations emerge. Intentional controls for security, rights or accepted commitments have a different basis. Assess an adaptation against those obligations before evaluating its economic benefit.

Likewise, retaining evidence for future questions remains subject to consent, appropriate purpose and retention. Retiring an index does not authorize deleting its source; preserving an original does not confer a right to disclose it. Storage location alone does not establish authority or privacy.

Read economic claims through the feasible boundary

Capability Economics first excludes paths that fail required quality, authority, safety or reliability. Its expected-value model does not replace hard constraints or make unacceptable downside acceptable. The paper's examples illustrate choices, rather than establishing a universal benefit from agents or a regulatory compliance result.

This guide identifies architectural questions. It does not interpret a jurisdiction's law. Applying the framework in a regulated setting requires the actual obligations and evidence relevant to that setting.

A short reading path

  1. Degrees of Decision Freedom. Understand delegation and retained human decisions.
  2. An Intelligence-Native Reference Architecture. Identify principal, evidence and governance boundaries.
  3. Parallel Realizations. Examine compatibility, adoption and retirement obligations.
  4. Continuous Intelligence, Adaptive Evolution, and Stable Experience. Read sections 4.3, 6.3, 9.2 and scenario D.

Questions to explore

  • Who can define the purpose, delegate actions, accept results and revise commitments?
  • Which choices are already authorized, and which require a reserved decision?
  • Who may review a signal, release an alternative, adopt it or retire an accepted version?
  • Which controls must remain enforceable across every permitted realization?

The research and education guide develops how such claims can be evaluated rather than assumed.

Resource foundations:
- Attributable information, accepted meaning and human decisions provide continuity while interfaces and implementations adapt.
- A capability is a meaningful ability with an accepted outcome. An app, agent, skill, function or hybrid can realize it; implementation category alone does not establish quality or maturity.
- Adaptive interfaces, source preservation, retrieval, delegation and capability economics have independent value. Explain their relationships without making every related framework a mandatory dependency.
- Begin with languages, formats and shared meaning. A schema may be generated, inferred or negotiated where useful. Accurately distinguish a proposed interaction from the guarantees of a specific claimed protocol implementation.
- Compare feasible strategies by cumulative net value, timing, full lifecycle costs and retained flexibility. Some quality, safety, authority and reliability requirements must be satisfied before first use.
- Source preservation and processing investment are distinct. Derived structures retain their provenance and do not acquire source authority through convenience or location.
- Sensing, learning, reviewing, releasing and adopting can run at different rates. Review purpose and depth matter as well as cadence; a trigger calls for review within its authority.
- Stable operations and familiar experiences have value. Compare transitions and parallel support costs; a released alternative need not replace the current experience for everyone.
- Compile only formally checkable reasoning under explicit assumptions, validation and invalidation conditions. Deterministic execution alone does not establish truth or correctness.
- Verify the beneficiary's outcome. Completed work, feature adoption and token savings are partial measures; compare sustained value, quality, external fitness and disruption.
