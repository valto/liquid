Liquid Software: a general introduction

Understand the foundations, how the ideas fit together and what remains to be tested.

Conceptual source: Valto Loikkanen · AI-assisted synthesis and research

Read source text ↗Give this to your agent

Liquid Software explores a digital world in which your information and intentions outlast the tools that happen to serve them. Instead of making every useful activity wait for a dedicated application, an intelligent system can interpret a need and use an appropriate existing, agentic, deterministic or hybrid way to fulfill it.

The central question is what should remain dependable when software can change more easily? The answer in this resource is attributable information, understandable meaning, accepted commitments and human authority. Interfaces and execution methods can adapt around those foundations.

Five ideas to carry into the papers

Information provides continuity. An appropriate original document can support a later question that its first summary did not anticipate. Preserve the distinction between evidence, an interpretation and a useful index. Ownership, consent and retention still govern what may be kept.

A capability has meaning beyond its implementation. Preparing a reliable commitments briefing is a meaningful ability. A screen, query or model call can help realize it. Replacing the screen should preserve what counts as an accepted commitment and whose information may be used.

Interaction can suit the recipient. A person might need a spoken explanation; an analyst a table; an agent structured text. Shared meaning can guide these presentations. A new format alone supplies neither agreement nor permission.

Software is a choice to evaluate. Repeated work can justify a validated function, cache or service. A changing task may remain intelligent. A critical control may need stable software from its first use. The proposed continuum allows all of these choices and allows them to be revisited.

Learning need not disturb what works. A system can notice changes and review them while keeping operations and your familiar workspace stable. A new version can be offered alongside the current one. Whether to adopt it depends on useful outcomes, transition costs and your applicable choices.

Imagine preparing tomorrow's briefing

You ask, “Which commitments need my attention tomorrow?” A bounded system identifies you, applies the agreed meaning of commitment, checks authorized source records and returns a suitable explanation with references and uncertainty. It might combine an exact date query with intelligent interpretation of notes.

If you ask often, a validated query or cache could become worthwhile. It might improve beneath the same familiar interface. A different presentation could remain an optional alternative. The records, commitments and actual outcome still matter. This is illustrative, rather than a deployed service description.

What changes for the reader

Ask first what outcome you want, what evidence it needs and what authority is available. Then ask which tool or combination can fulfill it acceptably. This helps separate a durable need from the features of whichever app currently handles it.

The related ideas can help separately: source preservation without generated interfaces, adaptive presentation with conventional software, or capability economics inside an existing organization. Understanding the resource does not require adopting every related framework.

What remains to be established

The resource is conceptual and AI-assisted. Its economic figures are illustrative. A fluent answer is not proof of correctness, and software generation does not establish reliability. Useful evaluation must compare accepted outcomes, cost, latency, permission compliance and recovery against real alternatives.

Resource foundations:
- Attributable information, accepted meaning and human decisions provide continuity while interfaces and implementations adapt.
- A capability is a meaningful ability with an accepted outcome. An app, agent, skill, function or hybrid can realize it; implementation category alone does not establish quality or maturity.
- Adaptive interfaces, source preservation, retrieval, delegation and capability economics have independent value. Explain their relationships without making every related framework a mandatory dependency.
- Begin with languages, formats and shared meaning. A schema may be generated, inferred or negotiated where useful. Accurately distinguish a proposed interaction from the guarantees of a specific claimed protocol implementation.
- Compare feasible strategies by cumulative net value, timing, full lifecycle costs and retained flexibility. Some quality, safety, authority and reliability requirements must be satisfied before first use.
- Source preservation and processing investment are distinct. Derived structures retain their provenance and do not acquire source authority through convenience or location.
- Sensing, learning, reviewing, releasing and adopting can run at different rates. Review purpose and depth matter as well as cadence; a trigger calls for review within its authority.
- Stable operations and familiar experiences have value. Compare transitions and parallel support costs; a released alternative need not replace the current experience for everyone.
- Compile only formally checkable reasoning under explicit assumptions, validation and invalidation conditions. Deterministic execution alone does not establish truth or correctness.
- Verify the beneficiary's outcome. Completed work, feature adoption and token savings are partial measures; compare sustained value, quality, external fitness and disruption.
