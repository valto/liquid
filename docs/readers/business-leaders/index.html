Liquid Software for business leaders

Examine earlier accepted value, durable organizational knowledge, delegation and the ability to change direction.

Conceptual source: Valto Loikkanen · AI-assisted synthesis and research

Read source text ↗Give this to your agent

Liquid Software changes the starting point for technology decisions: define the business capability and its required outcome, then select a feasible way to deliver it. An application, an agent and a hybrid workflow are possible realizations of that capability. Their usefulness depends on accepted results and the organization they serve.

For a business leader, the main question is how can we deliver value sooner while retaining the ability to change direction? This resource connects that question to durable knowledge, delegation and the full cost of implementation.

Manage the outcome and the retained knowledge

A capability such as preparing a compliant supplier comparison should have an accountable owner, accepted results and explicit commitments. The organization's supplier records, terminology and prior decisions should remain understandable even if the current application or agent is replaced.

That separation can reveal useful options: use an existing service, fulfill a changing request with available tools, specialize a recurring calculation, or retain a conventional process. Counting new applications or agents does not show whether the outcome improved.

Delegate with a clear decision boundary

Degrees of Decision Freedom describes how prior decisions establish room for subsequent choices. A team or agent can have broad freedom to find evidence and compare offers while a person retains authority to approve the purchase. A new interface or faster execution path should preserve that distinction.

This means leaders can define purpose, limits and escalation points without specifying every operational step. The organization still needs usable guidance, traceable evidence and a way to intervene when a reserved decision arises.

Consider a supplier briefing

In an illustrative workflow, an agent gathers authorized supplier information and drafts a comparison. Exact functions calculate totals; declared rules check policy; an authorized person handles exceptions and commitments. Recurring pieces may justify specialization while changing questions remain flexible.

The proposal is to evaluate this composition against the current accepted process. Quality and authority must be feasible from first use. A purchasing commitment should not be treated as authorized merely because the system prepared a recommendation.

Evaluate the transition as well as the running cost

Capability Economics compares cumulative net value over a relevant horizon. Include setup, assurance, supervision, time to accepted value, maintenance, adoption and restoration if the change is reversed. A lower price per execution can coexist with a worse overall investment.

The expanded economics paper's six-month example illustrates how earlier accepted benefits can change a comparison between staged strategies. Its figures are assumptions, not savings a business should expect. Released staff capacity also needs an identified use before it becomes a cash-saving claim.

A useful management discussion

Choose one capability with a real beneficiary and measurable acceptance criteria. Identify the current baseline, feasible alternatives, sources, accountable owner and decision boundary. Ask what would justify continuing, specializing, changing or stopping the new realization, and what must be preserved if it is withdrawn.

The framework does not establish that every business should become agent-first. Existing tools, stable controls and upfront development remain legitimate choices where their outcomes and constraints justify them.

A short reading path

  1. Capability Economics. Connect capability decisions to cumulative net value.
  2. Degrees of Decision Freedom. Separate delegated choices from reserved decisions.
  3. Liquid Software: The Economics of Execution — Version 3.0. Read pages 12–16 and 21 for staged value, transitions and procurement composition.
  4. Documentation as Executable Foundations. Keep business meaning and guidance durable across tools.

Questions to explore

  • Which outcome is delayed by waiting for a new application, and is earlier fulfillment feasible?
  • What organizational knowledge and commitments must survive a provider change?
  • Which decisions can be delegated, and which remain reserved?
  • What evidence would justify the next investment or a reversal?

For a more detailed cost comparison, continue with the finance and investment guide.

Resource foundations:
- Attributable information, accepted meaning and human decisions provide continuity while interfaces and implementations adapt.
- A capability is a meaningful ability with an accepted outcome. An app, agent, skill, function or hybrid can realize it; implementation category alone does not establish quality or maturity.
- Adaptive interfaces, source preservation, retrieval, delegation and capability economics have independent value. Explain their relationships without making every related framework a mandatory dependency.
- Begin with languages, formats and shared meaning. A schema may be generated, inferred or negotiated where useful. Accurately distinguish a proposed interaction from the guarantees of a specific claimed protocol implementation.
- Compare feasible strategies by cumulative net value, timing, full lifecycle costs and retained flexibility. Some quality, safety, authority and reliability requirements must be satisfied before first use.
- Source preservation and processing investment are distinct. Derived structures retain their provenance and do not acquire source authority through convenience or location.
