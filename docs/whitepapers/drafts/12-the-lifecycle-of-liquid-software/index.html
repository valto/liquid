Emergence, specialization, consolidation and retirement without losing capability meaning

Conceptual source and direction: Valto Loikkanen. Research and drafting: Codex (OpenAI), at his request. Research draft 1.0 · 26 November 2026.

Abstract

Liquid Software requires an operational lifecycle, not merely the ability to generate code. Valto Loikkanen proposes reversible movement among agentic, hybrid and deterministic realizations according to demand and constraints. This paper defines lifecycle states, transition evidence and the difference between retiring an implementation and withdrawing a capability. It addresses seasonal demand, dependency drift, user familiarity and preservation of accepted information. The resulting model makes adaptability manageable while allowing stable implementations to remain stable when they provide the best value. [S01]

1. A population of implementations

An information capability can have several realizations. A general agent may handle novel cases; a query may serve a stable pattern; a temporary interface may support a workshop; a hybrid route may handle exceptions.

Treating these as a population makes different lifetimes visible. Some implementations are short-lived, some recurring and some effectively permanent. The capability's identity and meaningful outcome can persist across them.

The lifecycle should not force a linear march from agent to software. Demand, information and constraints can change in either direction.

2. Capability lifecycle and implementation lifecycle differ

Retiring a specialized query can leave the capability available through another route. Withdrawing the capability means the system no longer promises the relevant outcome. These are different decisions.

A replacement must preserve the applicable contract or explicitly revise it. If the fallback is slower, less complete or less reliable, the system should determine whether it still meets the accepted capability conditions.

Accepted records, pending work and commitments also have their own lifecycles. Their retention cannot be inferred from the lifespan of the software that created or displayed them.

3. Proposed realization states

State Meaning Evidence needed to leave the state
Candidate Proposed response to an actual need Clear scope and expected value
Experimental Isolated construction or evaluation Relevant correctness and boundary evidence
Shadow Compared with an accepted route Adequate behavior on new cases
Active Available for a declared request class Current dependencies and runtime assurance
Restricted Temporarily limited or bypassed Cause resolved or scope explicitly revised
Retired No longer selected for execution Capability disposition and evidence retained

These states are a reference design, not a claim about the lifecycle of any existing PIOS component. A small temporary renderer may need fewer explicit stages than a shared state-changing service.

4. What justifies a transition?

Emergence follows an unmet or newly expressed need. Specialization follows demonstrated repeated value and adequate semantic stability. Consolidation follows evidence that several realizations share behavior without losing distinctions. Retirement follows declining value, superseded assumptions or an accepted replacement.

Demand alone is insufficient. The decision also depends on quality, latency, resource cost, security, source coverage, recovery and human effort. A rarely used critical route may remain valuable. A heavily used route may need restriction if its assumptions are wrong.

The optimizer should preserve the reason for a material transition. Without that history, future actors may repeat failed adaptations or mistake a retired artifact for a currently accepted implementation.

5. Identity, dependencies and acceptance

A realization should identify the capability and interaction version it supports, its own construction provenance, relevant dependencies and accepted operating scope. Code generated by an agent can still run deterministically; construction method does not determine runtime control.

CRD explicitly separates capability meaning, interaction contracts and realizations. Its runtime distinctions help prevent agent-built software from being treated as agent-controlled execution. [S09]

Acceptance evidence should follow the meaningful boundary. It can include source-backed cases, denied scope, changed source revisions and recovery. Repeat testing only when the relevant inputs or unresolved risks change; a renamed artifact alone does not establish a new correctness question.

6. Seasonal return and dependency drift

A annual reporting capability might use a specialized route for a short period. After the period, the route may retire from active operation while its source, scope and evidence remain available under retention policy.

When the need returns, reinstatement should check current terminology, source structure, permissions and operational dependencies. Historical success is useful evidence, but it may not prove compatibility with changed conditions.

The same applies to model-driven components. Changing an extraction model may alter derived assertions even if the source bytes remain unchanged. A realization must be evaluated against the requirement that actually depends on that model.

7. Stability is sometimes the optimum

Users learn interfaces and develop reliable habits. Continuous visible change can impose cognitive costs even when generation is cheap. A stable view may therefore remain preferred while the serving implementation evolves behind it.

Malleable-software research emphasizes users' ability to reshape their environment. That is compatible with choosing to keep a useful environment unchanged. The lifecycle should support control over adaptation, including the choice to retain a familiar form. [S04]

Likewise, a proven backend may remain cheaper than regenerating it. Liquid capability is the ability to adapt appropriately, not a requirement to demonstrate change at every opportunity.

8. Retirement and recovery

Before retirement, identify ongoing requests, external commitments, accepted state and pending work. Stop selecting the route through the supported mechanism; preserve the information required to understand and recover relevant outcomes.

Retirement should not silently delete originals or invalidate a client's agreed interaction. If the capability is being withdrawn, communicate its disposition through the appropriate decision and interaction process.

End-to-end reasoning is especially useful during replacement: a component may exit cleanly while a client still cannot determine whether its operation completed. The retained outcome and recovery boundary matter more than the component's local success. [S20]

9. Evaluation and open questions

Run a changing workload through several realizations. Compare stable service, selective adaptation and aggressive adaptation. Measure useful fulfillment, maintenance cost, regressions, user learning, fallback quality and recovery.

Test a withdrawal during work, a seasonal return, a capability revision and consolidation of superficially similar patterns. Determine whether accepted information and meaningful distinctions survive.

The contribution is a reversible lifecycle around durable capability meaning. It makes generation, specialization and retirement parts of one accountable process rather than independent acts of software creation and deletion.
