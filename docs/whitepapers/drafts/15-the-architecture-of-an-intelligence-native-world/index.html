A composable reference model for continuity, interaction, execution and human authority

Conceptual source and direction: Valto Loikkanen. Research and drafting: Codex (OpenAI), at his request. Research draft 1.0 · 26 November 2026.

Abstract

This paper synthesizes Valto Loikkanen's complementary concepts into a reference architecture. Individuals and entities provide persistent principals; information foundations preserve evidence and accepted state; documentation and CRDs supply capability meaning; adaptive interfaces express needs through suitable languages and formats; agentic, deterministic and hybrid realizations fulfill them; and an optimization loop responds to actual demand. Human decision freedom governs all layers. The architecture is a research proposal that can be adopted selectively, not a new mandatory standard or a claim that the complete system is already deployed. [S01]

1. What the architecture must accomplish

A useful architecture should let a principal preserve information, express an emerging need, obtain an attributable result and retain control over consequential effects. It should also permit changing the actor, interface or realization without unnecessarily losing capability meaning or accepted state.

These goals create a linked set of concerns. Preserving raw material without meaning is insufficient; shared meaning without accessible evidence is insufficient; flexible execution without authority is unacceptable; and stable information without usable presentation is of limited practical value.

The reference model connects these concerns while allowing existing systems to supply particular components.

2. The composable model

Concern Reference role Complementary concepts
Principal Individual or entity served by an interaction PIOS, EIOS, personal application ecosystems
Information continuity Originals, accepted revisions, history and provenance Capture for Unknown Future Value
Semantic foundation Terms, capabilities, rules, defaults and unknowns Documentation, CRD
Interaction Context, intent, language, format and result Adaptive Interface, optional PAIX/MCP/A2A exposures
Fulfillment Select and execute an appropriate realization Intelligence-first, Liquid Software
Access optimization Find and assemble information efficiently Adaptive Information Retrieval
Evolution Observe, specialize, revise and retire Self-Optimizing Information System
Human authority Set and revise the effective decision space Degrees of Decision Freedom

These are logical responsibilities, not mandatory separate machines or services. A small deployment may combine several; a distributed deployment may separate them. Physical placement should follow the actual framework and task boundary.

3. Principal and information foundations

PIOS supplies a public personal information framework; EIOS supplies a related entity framework. They distinguish durable information and governance from the changing tools used to access and apply it. Their published specifications are design sources and should not be treated as independent evidence of every proposed feature. [S07, S08]

The information foundation should preserve original identity, accepted revision and relevant source history. Derived assertions remain attributable. Structured originals and multimodal originals can coexist; the storage medium does not decide authority.

Operational actors may be humans, agents or applications. Their identities, represented principals and grants should remain distinguishable. A component's access or physical proximity does not make it a canonical writer.

4. Shared meaning constrains interpretation

Terminology and capability documentation define what participants mean and what a useful result must preserve. They can also provide defaults and methods for resolving ordinary unspecified details.

CRD is relevant because it separates complete capability meaning, interaction behavior and realization. It can connect human-facing and machine-facing forms without making an endpoint or tool the entire capability. [S09]

Unknowns must remain visible. An agent may infer a presentation choice within guidance while lacking authority to redefine a disputed business concept. The architecture needs an explicit route for a real new decision, alongside room for routine interpretation.

5. The interaction lifecycle

  1. A requester expresses a need using a suitable language or format.
  2. The receiver resolves context, represented principal, meaning and expected result.
  3. Existing documentation and defaults settle ordinary details where sufficient.
  4. The receiver identifies source availability and applicable authority.
  5. A suitable realization retrieves information or performs the authorized effect.
  6. The result preserves material evidence, uncertainty and accepted state.
  7. Selected operational observations inform later optimization.

This sequence is conceptual. A real implementation may combine steps or use an already accepted interaction. It should not introduce negotiation for details that existing shared foundations already resolve.

6. Languages, formats and emerging contracts

Natural language, structured queries, diagrams and machine messages are different ways to express or carry the interaction. Shared meaning can precede a dedicated interface.

A schema may be generated for a selected view, inferred under documented conventions or negotiated where exact consumption requires it. The requester proposes the desired information and form; the receiver resolves these against supported meaning, evidence and authority. Existing GraphQL conformance requires its applicable type-system and validation rules, while the broader architecture can support other languages and guarantees. [S11]

REST-style, MCP, A2A and event-driven exposures can provide useful interaction machinery. PAIX's guidance adds a proposed entry and fallback channel with scoped optional upgrades. None automatically grants authority or determines whether the serving logic is agentic or deterministic. [S10, S12–S16]

7. Fulfillment and reversible specialization

A dispatcher selects among acceptable realizations according to the request, current dependencies, quality, cost and latency. A novel request may require intelligent interpretation; a stable repeated operation may use direct software; a mixed request may combine them.

The optimizer observes actual use, proposes a bounded improvement, validates it and activates it within existing authority. Its goal is useful fulfillment, not maximum generated code or minimum latency alone.

Withdrawal preserves capability disposition and accepted information. A fallback must meet the relevant promise. A material change to the meaning or effects is a contract revision, not an invisible optimization.

8. Human sovereignty and representation

Human decisions establish the permitted operational space. Agents can perform many lower-level actions within that space without seeking a repeated instruction for each step. They should escalate material ambiguity, reserved effects and authority conflicts.

The architecture also needs to account for interests. A representative may depend on a provider whose economic incentives differ from the principal's. The conversation's Personal Intelligence Fiduciary idea points to loyal representation as a complementary institutional question. It is not solved by a message format or by calling an agent personal. [S01]

Useful sovereignty includes understandable delegation, source access, practical recovery and the ability to replace actors. Technical availability alone does not establish these conditions.

9. A minimal reference experiment

Select one read-only capability and an authorized source set. Preserve its originals, define meaningful results and offer two interaction forms. Serve requests through a general interpretation route and one conventional route where available.

Measure outcomes and observed demand. Specialize one repeated pattern, then compare it with the original route on new and exceptional cases. Test a source revision, denied scope, implementation withdrawal and reconstruction of the relevant derived state.

This experiment establishes a bounded mechanism. Wider deployment, state-changing capabilities, public interfaces and market interaction require their own evidence and applicable authority. They should not be claimed on the basis of the read-only experiment.

10. Evaluation and unresolved research

The architecture should be assessed through semantic fidelity, useful completion, total cost, human effort, permission preservation, freshness, portability and recovery. Each dimension can reveal failure hidden by an impressive first demonstration.

Open questions include interoperability across different terminologies, cost-effective validation of generated realizations, evolving delegation, conflict among principals and the economics of long-term support.

The contribution is a composable model whose components can be independently useful. Information supplies continuity, shared meaning supplies coherence, human decisions establish freedom, and adaptable interaction and execution serve the need. This is the connected synthesis attributed to Loikkanen, presented as an invitation to implementation, comparison and critical testing.
