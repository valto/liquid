A dynamic framework for when intelligence should act directly, when software should materialize, and when existing implementations should evolve or dissolve.

The economic question

Which feasible way of fulfilling a need delivers the greatest cumulative net value over the relevant time horizon?

The paper compares direct intelligent execution, reusable skills, deterministic functions, software modules, dedicated applications and combinations of these. It treats that choice as reversible. A deterministic implementation can be the right initial choice, remain useful for years, or later give way to another realization.

The objective includes time to value, full lifecycle expenditure, transition and restoration costs, failure risk, foregone opportunities and the value of retaining flexibility. Lower coding cost and lower inference cost each change the comparison; neither alone establishes the winner.

A decision framework, with evidence limits

The paper is a conceptual framework, not a measured demonstration that agents, local hardware or one vendor architecture always cost less. Its source note makes this limitation explicit.

Pendo's 2019 report describes feature usage in its own sampled products and an extrapolated R&D estimate. GAO's July 2026 Technology Modernization Fund review distinguishes cash-savings expectations from other benefits and examines a particular public-sector portfolio. The UK supply-chain study reports qualitative accounts of system adoption and a few reversals. These findings have different denominators and measures and must remain separate.

Stanford's historical inference-price comparison concerns approximate GPT-3.5-level MMLU performance, from November 2022 to October 2024. It does not measure total agent costs, owned-hardware economics or future prices. CRD provides a way to describe capabilities separately from realizations. MDIL/Kide's public claims illustrate a proposed modular architecture; they are not independently verified comparative benchmarks.

The cited GAO-11-53 page and Washington Post payroll article could not be read during this intake. The original claims remain attributable to the supplied PDF. An official Senate report independently corroborates ECSS cancellation after more than $1 billion was spent, but does not verify every detail of the inaccessible GAO status update.
