 01 | EXECUTIVE THESIS



 The economics of execution
 AI changes two things at once: the cost of making software and the need for software to exist. The first
 is widely understood. The second requires a different view of how individuals and organizations
 operate. If intelligence can interpret a need, exercise delegated authority, use available tools and deliver
 a result, a dedicated implementation is no longer an automatic prerequisite for accomplishing work.
 Liquid Software is an architectural and economic framework for deciding which parts of an activity
 should be performed by general-purpose intelligence, which should be realized in reusable
 deterministic functions, and which warrant durable software. It extends the public Liquid Software
 body of work by developing the economics of those choices over time.

 The central optimization objective is not to maximize AI use, minimize software, or minimize cost per
 transaction. It is to maximize expected cumulative net value over time, subject to quality, safety,
 authority, privacy, reliability and other non-negotiable requirements. The relevant unit is the
 meaningful capability, not necessarily an application or agent.


   Intelligence is the default capability. Software is an optimization.

 The framework brings together nine dimensions: execution cost, total lifecycle economics, uncertain
 adoption, transition costs, delayed value, the value of flexibility, changing model economics, modular
 reuse and information processing. Their interaction changes the investment sequence: fulfill needs
 where feasible, learn from actual execution, then specialize selectively. Software can remain stable for
 years when justified, or evolve and dissolve when it ceases to be the best realization.




             Information                    Human decisions                 Intelligence                     Software
              continuity                       authority                     fulfillment                    optimization




                                      Durable foundations → replaceable realizations


 Figure 1. The four enduring layers of the public Liquid Software framework. The optimization concerns realizations, not the
 withdrawal of human authority or information ownership.

 02 | INTRODUCTION AND SCOPE



 Why the question has changed
 In conventional computing, a business need is commonly translated into requirements, an application or
 integration is built, users adopt it, and benefits are expected to follow. This was often rational.
 General-purpose software did not independently interpret a novel request and carry it through to
 completion. The organization needed a predetermined mechanism to translate intent into reliable
 operations.
 AI-assisted coding improves the production side of this arrangement. An agent can generate a user
 interface, write the backend and test the resulting software in a fraction of the time previously
 required. Yet the resulting operating model can remain identical: people must use the software and
 conform to its supported behavior. Faster construction is not in itself a change in the reason for
 constructing the application.

 The deeper shift arises when an agent can fulfill the underlying need, with appropriate access, tools and
 authority. Instead of asking how to implement a requested application, the agent or decision-maker can
 ask which aspects of the need require an implementation at all. The answer may be a fully deterministic
 service, direct reasoning, existing software, or a changing composition of all three.


      SOFTWARE-FIRST

                     Business                                Human
                                                                                                   Software + AI
                     outcome                                workflow

      AGENT-FIRST

                     Business                               Agentic                               Tools, functions
                     outcome                              coordination                            or direct action


 Figure 2. Two operating models can use the same technology. The difference is which actor determines the means of execution.

 This paper concerns the economics of that shift for individuals and entities. It is not a forecast of the
 disappearance of software, a proposal to replace all human work, or an instruction to grant unrestricted
 permissions to agents. An organization can deliberately maintain existing tools or specialized systems
 because their safety, predictability or value justifies them. The framework is neutral about the eventual
 implementation, but not blind to emerging alternatives.

 03 | GENERAL-PURPOSE INTELLIGENCE



 The self-driving car and the humanoid
 A self-driving car is an excellent example of an intelligence-empowered tool. It may integrate sensors,
 perception, mapping, decision-making and vehicle control into a highly advanced system. The
 intelligence is directed toward making the vehicle drive. Its sophistication does not necessarily give it a
 useful role in a kitchen, on a bicycle or in an office.

 Now imagine a sufficiently capable general-purpose humanoid. It can drive a compatible car made
 decades before autonomous driving, operate a truck, ride a bicycle, prepare food and, where attention
 and safety permit, delegate or execute digital chores concurrently. The old car does not need to be
 rebuilt as a self-driving car. It becomes autonomously operated from the perspective of its owner by
 introducing an independent intelligent actor. This is a conceptual analogy, not a claim about current
 robotics performance.



                 Self-driving car                                                    One purpose
             Intelligence inside tool                                         Drive the integrated vehicle

                                                                                          Digital work

               General intelligence                                                     Bicycle / kitchen
                Independent actor
                                                                                         Old car / truck


 Figure 3. Tool-specific intelligence versus intelligence reusable across tools and purposes.

 The organizational parallel is direct. Instead of upgrading every existing application with its own AI
 layer, general-purpose agents can use available applications, interfaces and data under appropriate
 permissions. An enterprise with old tools and highly capable, well-governed agents could consequently
 operate more agentically than an enterprise whose applications all have embedded AI but whose
 workflows remain software-defined.

 This does not make the specialized car obsolete. For a fleet that performs the same transport activity at
 high utilization, the integrated autonomous vehicle may offer better safety, reliability or lifetime
 economics. The humanoid may be preferable for varied, irregular work. The choice becomes contextual
 rather than a universal ranking of technologies.


   Instead of making every tool intelligent, give intelligence access to every
   appropriate tool. Specialize where the requirements and economics justify it.

 04 | GENERALITY AND SPECIALIZATION



 Industrial robots, roads and houses
 An industrial robot is engineered for a defined class of work. A production line may invest in tooling,
 fixtures, calibration and a tightly controlled environment because millions of repeated operations justify
 the investment. A general-purpose humanoid can instead perform an occasional assembly task, use a
 specialized tool for one step, or work on a different activity entirely.

 The most instructive factory is one where capable intelligent actors can do the work, use specialized
 machines, and identify opportunities to create new machinery when repetition and performance justify
 it. They can also change the composition of the production line or retire a machine. In the digital
 counterpart, agents do not need an entire application for a single deterministic calculation; they may
 only need a function. They can use the same function in multiple otherwise unrelated activities.


   “You don’t build a house for one night visit or road to walk by once.”
   Valto Loikkanen, November 2026


                  Existing exit                                                    New destination




                                                             Missing exit


                                    A road optimizes movement only along supported routes.


 Figure 4. Infrastructure increases efficiency on a chosen route. Its value depends on how often the route is useful and whether
 alternative destinations remain reachable.

 A road is a further illustration of specialization. Repeated journeys can justify improving a path, building
 a road and eventually adding more capacity. But a road without appropriate exits can constrain travel.
 A new destination may require leaving the road or taking a long detour. The cost of the road includes
 the cost of the options it removes, not merely its construction and upkeep.

 The analogies are not absolute: specialized robots can use adaptive AI, general-purpose actors can
 follow deterministic routines, and shared roads or accommodation may already exist. That is precisely
 the point. The relevant economic choice is between available realizations, including reuse of
 infrastructure built by others. One visit can justify renting a room; it rarely justifies constructing a house
 for that visit alone.

 05 | THE ECONOMIC BOUNDARY



 From SaaS economics to entity economics
 Historically, the economics of SaaS made many narrow applications viable. A vendor could invest in
 product design, implementation, integrations, testing, sales, support and ongoing maintenance, then
 spread fixed costs over thousands of paying customers. Each individual customer could obtain
 functionality for a price well below the cost of building and maintaining an equivalent private
 implementation.
 When an individual or organization can fulfill needs directly through intelligence and generate
 dedicated software on demand, the comparison changes. The relevant demand for a privately built
 capability is its actual and expected use inside that entity, including reuse across distinct internal
 activities. A million potential users elsewhere do not amortize a private implementation unless there is a
 credible external distribution model.




              Shared SaaS product                                                                  Private capability




       Org 1      Org 2       Org 3         Org 4                                        Need A        Need B        Need C

                                                    Different amortization boundaries

 Figure 5. Traditional shared-product amortization and entity-level amortization are different investment boundaries. Existing
 shared SaaS remains an eligible option.

 Consider an organization that needs a particular report twice a year. A commercial report tool may
 make economic sense because it is already built and subscribed to. A private reporting application
 might not. Direct execution by an agent using the existing sources could deliver the report without
 designing an interface, defining an entire data model or maintaining a new application for the
 intervening months. If demand expands into weekly reporting across multiple functions, a reusable
 query or report-generation module may then justify itself.

 This is not an argument against commercial software. Buying, renting, consuming an API, using a local
 function and executing with a model are all realizations. A vendor's economies of scale may still make
 SaaS the superior choice. A reusable private component can similarly gain scale through repeated use
 within one entity. The framework moves the unit of analysis from the assumed application market to
 the actual need and the available cost-sharing arrangements.

 06 | THE EXECUTION CONTINUUM



 Neither agents nor software are the
 endpoint
 The phrase “agents determine whether software is needed at all” should not be read as a binary
 contest. A single activity can combine open-ended reasoning, reusable instructions, small models,
 deterministic functions, existing APIs and dedicated software. Each constituent capability can occupy a
 different position on a continuum of execution freedom and predefined behavior.




             Direct                    Reusable              Deterministic             Software                 Dedicated
             agentic                     skills               functions                modules                  software




                                            Greater specialization is optional and reversible


 Figure 6. The continuum is reversible. Reusable skills may themselves combine reasoning and deterministic logic; the categories
 are illustrative rather than rigid stages.

 A novel task may start with direct agentic execution. Repeated success may make the method worth
 preserving as a reusable skill. The agent can continue to interpret the situation but call a validated
 calculation function for a stable subtask. More stable and frequently composed functions may become
 a module. An entire software service may be justified when the full activity is sufficiently predictable or
 requires stronger guarantees.
 The direction is not necessarily toward software. Requirements can change, usage can decline, a
 cheaper model can become available, or a fixed workflow can become a barrier. A module can be
 removed from the active path and replaced with intelligent execution while preserving the original
 information and accepted commitments. Some implementations should remain deterministic
 indefinitely, particularly where predictability is essential.

 As an example, a procurement request might require contextual interpretation of a new business need,
 extraction of fields with a local model, exact calculation of totals, evaluation of declared purchasing
 rules and submission through an existing procurement service. The application as a whole need not be
 rebuilt, and the same agent need not use the same model for every part of the task. This becomes
 practical only when the necessary interfaces and governance allow those parts to be composed
 independently.

 The measure of maturity is the ability to make that composition deliberately, not the proportion of
 execution assigned to one end of the continuum.

 07 | LIFECYCLE ECONOMICS



 The cost of existence
 Runtime cost is only one part of the comparison. Dedicated software can be extremely cheap per
 invocation while being expensive to design, test, deploy, maintain and eventually remove. Agentic
 execution also incurs costs: configuration, permissions, model and tool access, assurance, exception
 handling and maintenance of context and runtime. A valid comparison includes all incremental costs
 needed to deliver the same capability.

    Lifecycle stage                    Agentic realization                       Dedicated realization

    Design                             Goals, context, constraints, acceptance   Requirements, architecture, interaction
                                                                                 design

    Build and deploy                   Configure skills, tools, authority and    Implement, integrate, test, migrate and
                                       runtime                                   release

    Execute                            Inference, retries, tool calls and        Compute, hosting and operational
                                       supervision                               support

    Assure                             Evaluate results, verify evidence,        Tests, monitoring, security and controls
                                       manage errors

    Adapt and maintain                 Refresh context, models, skills and       Update dependencies, code, interfaces
                                       access                                    and rules

    Retire                             Withdraw realization; preserve evidence   Decommission, reconcile and migrate as
                                                                                 required

 The distinction between fixed and variable costs is important. A rarely used integration may need
 security patches and compatibility upgrades even in months when it is not called. A dedicated user
 interface may require maintenance as browsers or organizational roles change. Some of these costs
 arise from the continued existence of the implementation, rather than its use. A shared module can
 spread these obligations across several needs, but it can also create a larger dependency surface.
 An intelligent path is not automatically free of fixed costs. An existing organizational agent runtime may
 already have absorbed some setup cost; a new one may need substantial investment. Only attributable
 incremental costs should be compared for the decision in question, while shared infrastructure costs
 must be allocated consistently at the portfolio level.


   Software can be inexpensive to execute while remaining expensive to own.

 08 | BREAK-EVEN MODEL



 When does software pay for itself?
 A simple model makes the tradeoff tangible. Let F be the incremental fixed lifetime cost of materializing
 a function, including design, build, deployment and predictable maintenance over the chosen horizon.
 Let a be the agentic cost per successful execution and s the corresponding software cost. For equal
 outcomes and a constant demand of N executions, direct execution costs aN and software costs F +
 sN. When a exceeds s, the simplified break-even is N* = F / (a - s). If a is less than or equal to s, this
 cost-only model supplies no positive usage threshold at which dedicated software wins.

    EUR               Agentic: €2.50/use                                   Software: €1,200 + €0.10/use




           2k

                                                                    500

           1k




            0
                0                           250                      500                         750                         1000
                                                                                                          Successful executions

 Figure 7. Illustrative inputs: F = €1,200, a = €2.50 and s = €0.10. Break-even occurs at 500 successful executions. No external
 empirical cost estimate is implied.

 For example, at 100 executions the intelligent path costs €250, compared with €1,210 for the
 dedicated implementation. At 1,000 executions those costs are €2,500 and €1,300 respectively. A
 rational organization may therefore use an existing agent for early needs, observe demand and
 materialize when the remaining expected benefit exceeds the incremental future cost.

 The threshold is not a trigger to count historical usage and automatically build at execution 500. Past
 expenditures are sunk; the decision at any date concerns future demand, remaining lifetime, migration
 cost and future alternatives. Quality, latency, failures, maintenance changes and risk must be
 normalized. If a capability is legally or operationally required to be deterministic, the intelligent path
 may not be eligible at all. Conversely, if deterministic software cannot handle important exceptions, an
 apparently attractive cost crossover may never produce comparable value.

 This deliberately simple model isolates execution economics. The remaining chapters explain why it is
 necessary but insufficient for a real investment decision.

 09 | INVESTMENT UNCERTAINTY



 Software that never creates expected
 value
 There is a material difference between a function that is used often, one that is used rarely, a project
 that never reaches production and an implementation that is adopted but fails to deliver its business
 case. All can be technically well designed. Their economics depend on what actually happens after
 investment, not what the project plan predicted.

    Category                                What is lost

    Never deployed                          Build expenditure and business time; possible salvage value of code or learning

    Deployed but underused                  Build and upkeep with insufficient realized demand; adoption effort

    Used but underdelivering                Expected improvement fails to materialize; opportunity and operating losses

    Introduced then reversed                Forward and reverse transition costs, possible data and customer consequences

 Evidence from public-sector audits and commercial product usage research documents these
 categories, although the available statistics measure different populations and cannot be combined into
 a worldwide failure rate. Pendo's 2019 Feature Adoption Report found that 80% of features in the
 average product in its dataset were rarely or never used; its extrapolation associated up to $29.5 billion
 in public-cloud R&D investment with underutilized features [1]. This is vendor research about features,
 not a percentage of projects that failed.
 The US Government Accountability Office (GAO) reported that four of six completed projects assessed
 for savings under the Technology Modernization Fund missed or were off track for expected savings as
 of June 2025; seven of 68 reviewed projects had been cancelled and no longer expected savings [2]. In
 a separate case, the US Air Force cancelled its Expeditionary Combat Support System after
 approximately $1 billion had been expended [3]. These are government findings about specific
 programs, not representative estimates for all software.
 The economic lesson is that the probability of non-use or underdelivery belongs in the decision before
 construction begins. A usage forecast should be treated as a scenario, not an entitlement to future
 value. Where direct execution is feasible, the organization can learn about real demand before making a
 permanent commitment.

 10 | EVIDENCE-DRIVEN INVESTMENT



 The value of not building
 An agent-first approach changes the sequence of learning and spending. Instead of predicting demand,
 building a complete system, then discovering whether users adopt it, an organization can first serve a
 need through an existing capability or intelligent execution. Actual usage produces information about
 frequency, variations, exceptions, cost and the value of the result. Those observations can justify only
 the portions that benefit from specialization.


     COMMIT FIRST

                     Predict                                  Design and                                  Deploy and
                     demand                                     build                                    seek adoption

     EVIDENCE FIRST

                       Serve                                   Observe                                     Specialize
                     the need                               actual demand                                  selectively


 Figure 8. The evidence-first sequence is conditional on a safe and useful initial execution path. It does not prohibit upfront
 infrastructure investment.

 This is economically related to the idea of real options: retaining the option to defer, expand, change or
 abandon an investment can have value under uncertainty. The intelligence-enabled path makes that
 option practical for a wider set of needs. The cost of waiting is not always zero, and postponement is
 not always optimal. There may be urgent capacity, regulatory or performance requirements that justify
 construction in advance. But the default assumption that a new need warrants its own application
 becomes less defensible.

 Consider an annual request for an unusual financial analysis. The organization could commission a
 dashboard because a future audience might use it. Alternatively, an authorized agent could produce the
 analysis, retain its evidence and record the request. If the analysis is never requested again, the
 dedicated dashboard was unnecessary. If it becomes a weekly decision input, a validated query or a
 report module can be developed with a much clearer specification. Learning was obtained while
 delivering value, not through an isolated software discovery phase.
 This also changes how success is measured. A software delivery team may be rewarded for completed
 implementations, while the entity benefits from the elimination of unnecessary projects. The optimizer
 must be evaluated on business outcomes and avoided waste, not on how many applications or agents it
 produces.


   The greatest economic benefit of Liquid Software may not be cheaper software,
   but all the software that never needs to be created.

 11 | TRANSITION ECONOMICS



 Double transition cost
 A human-facing software implementation is often also an organizational transition. Users must learn a
 new interface, adopt new terminology, change procedures, migrate information and coordinate with
 others who are making the same shift. Even a successful transition may temporarily depress
 productivity. If the implementation fails and the organization returns to its former way of working, a
 second transition follows.



                     Original                                 New                                    Restored
                    operations                              software                                operations




                         Forward disruption                                         Reverse disruption
                     Migration, training, downtime                               Restoration, reconciliation



 Figure 9. Both movements create costs. The second may require restoring operations that cannot be reconstructed by simply
 reinstalling the previous application.

 Forward costs can include training, management attention, parallel operation, data conversion, revised
 permissions, changed roles and disruption to customers. Reverse costs can include retraining,
 reconciliation of transactions, restoration of integrations, re-establishing procedures and repairing
 confidence in the organization's ability to change. Some of these costs may persist long after the
 technical rollback.

 UK government qualitative research in 2023 reported instances of businesses abandoning supply-chain
 software after replacement systems proved unsuitable or worse than previous arrangements [4]. A
 historical Washington Post report described Washington, D.C. spending more than $34 million in its
 unsuccessful payroll modernization and return to its original system [5]. The latter is a reported case,
 not an official audit estimate; neither source supports a general transition-cost multiplier.

 In an agent-first organization the transition may be less visible to human users when a stable capability
 contract and interaction remain unchanged while its underlying realization changes. An agent may
 switch from direct calculation to a validated function without requiring a department to learn another
 application. This is a potential advantage, not a guarantee: data migrations, authority changes, training
 and operational safeguards may still be necessary. Design for reversibility, and measure the actual
 disruption rather than assuming it disappears.

 12 | TIME AND OPPORTUNITY



 Business value before software
 Time spent waiting for a system is not merely an expense in the software budget. The business may
 lose revenue, defer a service, miss a market window or fail to make an important decision while the
 implementation is being built. Staff, management attention and capital may be diverted from other
 productive uses. When the original business need can be met directly, software development need not
 be on the critical path to value creation.


     SOFTWARE-FIRST

                 Design                          Build                          Deploy                           Value


     AGENT-FIRST (WHERE FEASIBLE)

                  Setup                          Value                           Value                           Value


                              Function A                        Module B                           Function C


 Figure 10. Software-first and agent-first sequences can reach the same final realization at different times in their value journeys.
 Agent setup and assurance are not necessarily instantaneous.

 Imagine a customer opportunity that requires an unusual analysis and proposal. A traditional approach
 may involve requesting changes to a reporting or CRM system, waiting for prioritization and
 development, and completing the work when the new functionality is available. If an authorized agent
 can retrieve the existing information, perform the analysis and prepare the proposal today, it may
 capture the opportunity without waiting. The relevant software component can be considered
 afterward if the activity recurs.
 The value of an earlier result varies by domain. A delayed internal report may have little impact; a lost
 customer or an expired procurement deadline may be irreversible. A model that compares only
 implementation expenditure and operating costs overlooks these consequences.

 The same logic applies to failed projects. Even if the original investment is refunded or some code is
 salvaged, the time lost cannot necessarily be recovered. An organization may have spent months
 optimizing an unsuccessful implementation rather than responding to customers or developing a
 different capability. Opportunity cost is the value of the best feasible alternative forgone, not the sum
 of all imaginable opportunities.


   Software development time is not merely a development cost. It is also
   potentially lost business time.

 13 | CUMULATIVE BUSINESS VALUE



 Identical software, different economic
 outcomes
 A worked example illustrates why the decision objective cannot be limited to lifecycle cost
 minimization. Assume two approaches ultimately use the same €12,000 deterministic module. The
 module generates €5,000 per month in gross business contribution and costs €500 per month to run.
 The conventional build takes three months. The alternative can serve the need during those three
 months with an existing agentic environment, delivering €4,000 in monthly contribution at €1,500 in
 monthly operating and assurance costs. Assume the build expense is incurred at the start in both cases;
 ignore discounting and other costs for illustration.

    First six months                   Software-first                                    Agent-first then same module

    Build investment                   €12,000                                           €12,000

    Months 1-3 net                     €0                                                €7,500
    operations

    Months 4-6 net                     €13,500                                           €13,500
    operations

    Six-month cumulative net           €1,500                                            €9,000
    value


    EUR               Software-first                                       Agent-first



           6k


            0


           -6k


          -12k
                 0                 1                2                 3                    4                5                  6
                                                                                                                         Months

 Figure 11. Illustrative cumulative net value. Both paths use the same module from month four. Earlier operations create a lasting
 €7,500 difference in this scenario.

 The agent-first path has additional operating costs during the build, yet it creates more cumulative
 value because it serves real needs while the other approach is waiting. The example deliberately
 assumes the same software is eventually built; it does not rely on avoiding development altogether. In
 practice, observation could also reduce scope, change the design or eliminate the need for the module.
 Conversely, early agentic execution could be too costly, risky or inaccurate to justify.

 Discounting can increase the value of early benefits, and time-sensitive opportunities may amplify it
 further. Decision-makers should measure actual contribution, not simply the number of tasks
 completed or hours saved. Released employee capacity is not automatically cash; the entity must be
 able to convert it into avoided hiring, profitable throughput or another defined benefit. This distinction
 prevents a faster activity from being mistaken for a better business outcome.

 14 | FLEXIBILITY ECONOMICS



 Software can be a constraint
 Software encodes assumptions about what work is, what information matters, who performs it and
 which sequence of operations is valid. Those assumptions are valuable where predictability is needed.
 They can also prevent an intelligent actor from discovering a better route to the same outcome. A fixed
 workflow can require a customer request to fit a prescribed process even when another route would be
 more suitable.
 This is the economic significance of the road analogy. A highway improves efficiency between
 supported destinations. If there are too few exits, the traveller must take a detour or cannot reach a
 new destination. A large application can behave similarly: a single interface and tightly coupled backend
 may expose only a subset of the actions the organization could legitimately perform.


     FIXED WORKFLOW


                 Input                         Step A                          Step B                        Outcome


     COMPOSABLE CAPABILITIES

                                            Function A
               Need                                                         Agent judgment                      Outcome
                                            Function B

 Figure 12. A monolithic prescribed path limits alternatives; independently accessible functions enable approved alternative
 compositions. Guardrails still govern which routes are permitted.

 Premature determinism is the act of fixing an implementation before the relevant variation, exceptions
 and potential evolution are sufficiently understood. It creates a hidden liability: changing a business
 decision can become conditional on changing software. A novel customer segment, new data source or
 policy exception may be impossible to support without a development project.

 Flexibility therefore has economic value even when there is no immediate plan to exercise it. A
 frequently used, low-cost implementation can still be inferior if it blocks valuable opportunities. The
 value of preserving alternatives depends on the likelihood and consequence of future change, not on
 architectural elegance alone.
 The aim is not unlimited discretion. Some boundaries must be enforced deterministically, particularly
 security, financial authority and safety. Distinguish intentional constraints (what must remain true) from
 incidental constraints (what an implementation happens to support). The former are durable; the latter
 should be open to reconsideration.


   Materialize determinism only where its benefits exceed its lifecycle costs and
   the value of the flexibility it removes.

 15 | DYNAMIC EXECUTION ECONOMICS



 The moving economic threshold
 The relative cost of agentic and deterministic execution is not fixed. Model capabilities, inference
 efficiency, hardware performance, quantization, runtime tooling and software-generation methods are
 evolving. A decision justified at one point in time can cease to be justified without any change in the
 underlying business need.

 Stanford's 2025 AI Index reports that the inference price for a model at approximately GPT-3.5-level
 performance on MMLU fell from $20 to $0.07 per million tokens between November 2022 and
 October 2024, a decline of more than 280-fold [6]. This is a historical comparison for a specific
 benchmark level. It does not establish a uniform trend for complex agents, nor does it guarantee future
 cost reductions. A task may require more tokens, tool calls, retries, advanced capabilities or supervision
 than a benchmark suggests.

 Agentic costs of €2.50, €1.30 and €0.50 imply thresholds of 500, 1,000 and 3,000 executions respectively.

 The implications are considerable. If a capability is likely to be used 700 times in its useful life, the first
 price environment may justify software; the later price environments may not. A decision based on
 current retail model prices alone can therefore overestimate the long-term economic benefit of
 specialization.

 However, development cost is also falling. If AI-assisted creation cuts the fixed cost of the same
 validated function from €1,200 to €240, software becomes viable at fewer executions, all else being
 equal. Both curves can move. Quality improvements, changing risk, integration costs and reuse may
 shift the comparison further. The point is not to predict which side will win but to avoid freezing today's
 economics into a permanent architecture.
 An adaptive organization should periodically reassess its expensive or highly constrained realizations,
 including those created when inference was more costly. No useful commitment should be withdrawn
 merely because a new model is cheaper; a candidate must demonstrate comparable outcomes within
 its required constraints.

 16 | LOCAL INTELLIGENCE



 Retail API pricing is not the whole market
 Many evaluations of agentic execution use the price of tokens purchased from a hosted provider. That
 is one realization of intelligence, not the intrinsic cost of intelligence. A capable model running on
 owned or otherwise available hardware can change the cost structure from a predominantly variable
 retail charge to a combination of fixed capacity and relatively low marginal electricity and operating
 costs.
 Consider an entity that already maintains suitable hardware. An infrequent local classification task may
 incur little incremental cash expenditure. A heavily used model might instead justify purchasing
 additional hardware. For an entity with low utilization, limited operations expertise or demanding
 performance needs, a hosted service may still be cheaper overall. Local inference does not mean zero
 cost, zero risk or equivalent model quality.

    Cost dimension                     Hosted inference                           Local or owned infrastructure

    Capacity                           Provider supplies compute; usage or        Hardware purchase or rental,
                                       subscription fees                          amortization, idle capacity

    Marginal cost                      Tokens, requests, tools, network or        Energy, wear, scheduling and
                                       service tiers                              incremental operations

    Assurance                          Provider controls plus entity evaluation   Entity manages model validation,
                                       and access                                 updates and security

    Performance                        Network and provider latency; elastic      Hardware limits, concurrency and
                                       scale                                      available memory

 A useful measure is cost per successful capability execution, including retries, verification and human
 review. A low-cost local model that cannot reliably extract the required facts may cost more than a
 higher-priced model that succeeds on the first attempt. Equally, a premium model used for a routine
 classification can be an unnecessary expenditure.

 For modular systems, it is possible to route only the hardest portion of a task to a large hosted model
 and use small local models or deterministic components for other portions. This can change the
 economics of an entire workflow without changing its external commitments. Data residency and
 privacy can also influence the choice, but must be evaluated against the actual deployment and
 controls rather than inferred from the word “local.”

 The economic claim is a conditional one: access to capable, lower-cost intelligence extends the space in
 which direct execution is viable. Whether that advantage materializes must be measured for the
 entity's workloads.

 17 | MODULARITY



 Optimize every layer independently
 The execution continuum is only useful at scale if the organization can change one part of a realization
 without replacing everything around it. Modularity is therefore not a secondary engineering
 preference. It is the central economic enabler of Liquid Software.

    Level                                   Independent economic question

    Entity                                  Which capabilities are needed, and for whose benefit?

    Activity                                Which capabilities should be composed for this outcome?

    Agent                                   Which responsibilities need an intelligent actor, for how long?

    Capability                              Which implementation best satisfies the CRD?

    Model                                   What level of intelligence is sufficient for this subtask?

    Software                                What should be fixed in a reusable function or module?

    Information                             What should be preserved, processed or derived, and when?

    Infrastructure                          Where and on what resources should execution occur?

 Traditional applications bundle a wide range of capabilities into one product. The bundle can be
 economically advantageous when its users share the same workflows. It can also make the entity buy,
 build or maintain many components that are not useful to a specific activity. An agent may only need a
 precise calculation, a reliable identity check or a way to submit a transaction. These are smaller units of
 investment than an application.
 The reuse boundary matters. A function for extracting invoice totals might also support budgeting and
 procurement, and a validated calculation may be reused across hundreds of different reports. Scale
 arises from use across needs, not only use across customers. The fixed cost of testing and maintaining
 the component can be shared, provided its semantics are genuinely common.

 Modularity itself has a cost. Interfaces, discovery, dependency management, validation and versioning
 can create overhead. Excessive fragmentation may be harder to operate than a coherent module. A
 useful unit is the smallest one with a meaningful independent purpose and a sufficiently stable contract,
 not simply the smallest unit of code. The CRD and Minimum Logical Element (MLE) concepts provide
 scope disciplines for identifying such units [7, 9].

 18 | CAPABILITY CONTRACTS



 The CRD separates what from how
 The Capability Requirements Document (CRD) is a stable description of what must be accomplished,
 independent of a particular model, application, endpoint or interface. It helps preserve meaning while
 the implementation changes. The canonical framework is available at valto.github.io/crd/ [7]; this paper
 uses it as a basis for economic comparison rather than redefining its specification.


                                                              CRD
                                                       Required capability


                                                    Quality, cost, authority




        Large model            Local model           Skill          Rules / MDIL           Software           Existing API


 Figure 14. A durable capability description can have multiple candidate realizations. A selection mechanism compares them
 against requirements and authority, not only cost.

 A CRD can specify the intended outcome, inputs and outputs, relevant terminology, constraints,
 acceptance criteria and failure behavior. An associated governance layer determines the authority of
 the actor performing the task. Documentation of a capability does not itself grant permission to
 exercise it.

 An implementation can be an advanced reasoning model, a smaller local model, a reusable agent skill, a
 deterministic software function, a declarative decision component, an existing API or a composition of
 these. These are not mutually exclusive categories. A capability may use intelligence to interpret an
 input and deterministic mechanisms to enforce a boundary.

 MDIL/Kide-type components are relevant examples of possible decision-logic mechanisms in this
 spectrum. The vendor describes separation of declared deterministic logic from language-model
 interpretation [8]. Such a mechanism may be evaluated for well-specified decisions where traceability
 and predictability matter, rather than assuming a general-purpose model or a bespoke application is
 required. This paper does not establish the performance, cost or completeness of a particular vendor
 implementation. Equivalent mechanisms, including ordinary rule engines and tested code, remain
 alternatives.
 The CRD enables a replacement to be tested against a stable meaning. It does not guarantee that all
 realizations are interchangeable: differences in supported inputs, evidence, uncertainty, authority and
 performance must be surfaced and evaluated. The investment decision follows the capability
 requirements, not the other way around.

 19 | A WORKED MODULAR COMPOSITION



 One activity, multiple realizations
 Consider an entity receiving a request to purchase equipment. Its purpose is to fulfill a legitimate
 business need while remaining within financial authority and procurement policy. A conventional
 software project might attempt to encode the entire request-to-purchase flow into a new application.
 The agent-first alternative starts with the underlying capabilities and composes suitable existing or
 newly materialized realizations.



                  Clarify need                             Extract fields                         Check policy
                Reasoning model                             Local model                           Rules / MDIL




                      Record                                  Commit                             Compute total
                  Evidence store                            Existing API                         Exact function

                                            Authority and acceptance apply to every step.

 Figure 15. Example decomposition. The model or technology choice can change per capability while the approved activity and
 records remain coherent.

 The initial need may be ambiguous. A general-purpose model can clarify the request and propose
 options. A smaller local model can extract structured fields from quotations when evaluation
 demonstrates adequate accuracy. A declared rule or deterministic decision component can check policy
 conditions against validated facts. An exact function computes totals. The approved purchase is
 submitted through an existing procurement service, and an authoritative record of the decision is
 retained. A human with the required authority resolves exceptions or approves commitments where
 necessary.

 Suppose classification volume grows but procurement policy changes frequently. It may make sense to
 stabilize field extraction or use a cheaper validated model while keeping interpretation of unusual
 needs agentic. A deterministic control that enforces a spending limit should not be weakened because
 another part of the task becomes cheaper. Rules and permissions are versioned separately from the
 execution path.
 If the entity later changes its procurement system, it may replace only the transaction adapter. If a new
 local model satisfies the classification CRD at lower total cost, it can replace the prior model without
 rebuilding the workflow. If a policy change renders a module obsolete, the optimizer can propose
 retirement. Each replacement requires testing, a controlled rollout and an acceptable recovery path.

 This example illustrates why the decision unit matters: the economics of an entire application or an
 entire agent conceal the economically distinct sub-capabilities that comprise the work.

 20 | ECONOMICS OF INFORMATION PROCESSING



 Preserve first, derive for a purpose
 The same logic that questions software formation also questions the automatic preprocessing of
 information. An entity may have vast amounts of source material: emails, documents, conversations,
 sensor records and other evidence. Traditional data architectures often encourage transformation into
 predefined structures, summaries, taxonomies and indexes before specific retrieval needs arise. This
 can be valuable, but processing everything because it is available can produce expensive derived
 artifacts that nobody ever uses.
 Liquid Software distinguishes durable information from the mechanisms that make particular questions
 easy to answer. The principle of Capture for Unknown Future Value emphasizes preserving appropriate
 source richness within authorized boundaries [9]. A summary produced for today's purpose should not
 silently replace the source evidence needed for tomorrow's different question. Yet preserving
 information does not require deriving every possible representation in advance.




            Original                 On-demand                  Cached                Selective                Extensive
            sources                  processing                 results             preprocessing            preprocessing




                                            Greater specialization is optional and reversible


 Figure 16. A spectrum from original information to extensive preprocessing. Derived structures can evolve independently of
 preserved evidence.

 The default economic question is whether a particular transformation is justified by a known purpose
 or by credible expected retrieval needs. If no such need exists, defer discretionary processing, while still
 performing necessary ingestion, basic discoverability, protective controls and retention management.
 When a question arises, intelligence can interpret the request and select an appropriate retrieval
 method. Repetition may then justify caching, indexing, extraction or a stable structured representation.
 There are important exceptions. Some preprocessing is essential for searchability, accessibility, security,
 response time, legal compliance or a predictable operational commitment. A high-volume service with
 strict latency requirements may need precomputed representations even before all queries have been
 observed. The framework calls for a justification, not an absolute prohibition.

 The cost of processing includes more than initial computation: derived information must be stored, kept
 fresh, reconciled, access-controlled, deleted when appropriate and understood in relation to its source.
 Its continued existence can impose obligations just as software does.

 22 | THE OPERATING LIFECYCLE



 Materialize, stabilize, adapt, dissolve
 Liquid Software describes a reversible lifecycle rather than continuous regeneration for its own sake.
 The entity starts with a need and selects an authorized realization. Actual execution supplies evidence
 about demand, success, cost, latency and exceptions. An optimization mechanism identifies candidates
 for specialization, adaptation, consolidation or retirement. Humans or delegated actors evaluate and
 approve changes according to their authority.




                Fulfill need                              Observe results                               Evaluate options


                                                 Economics + authority + evidence


              Retire or retain                           Validate / operate                            Materialize / adapt



 Figure 18. The lifecycle loops back to execution and observation. Retiring a realization is not permission to abandon a promised
 capability.

 Materialization may involve producing a tested deterministic function, extracting a reusable skill,
 constructing a dedicated module or caching derived information. Stabilization is equally legitimate. If a
 realization continues to fulfill its purpose efficiently and reliably, unnecessary change can destroy value
 through review overhead and new failures. Liquidity is the ability to change when useful, not the
 requirement to change often.

 Dissolution means retiring an implementation that is no longer economically or operationally justified. It
 does not mean deleting source evidence, accepted commitments, audit records or the semantic
 definition of the capability. Some components can be retained inactive for reuse; others require secure
 decommissioning. When a dependency is removed, affected callers must have an acceptable fallback or
 an explicitly revised commitment.

 The optimization process itself has costs. It must not spend more generating and assessing new
 realizations than those changes can reasonably return. Review triggers can include high repeated
 inference costs, a new model, lower demand, persistent exceptions, source changes or a significant
 change in business strategy. Scheduled review is one possible organization of the process; it is not a
 mandatory nightly rebuild.

 The enduring architecture is information, human decisions, agreed meaning and capabilities. The
 software, agents, models and retrieval mechanisms serving them remain replaceable within those
 boundaries.

 23 | A FORMAL DECISION OBJECTIVE



 Maximize risk-adjusted cumulative net
 value
 A practical economic model compares candidate realizations of the same capability or outcome over a
 relevant time horizon. It considers alternative demand and technology scenarios, rather than one
 forecast. Non-negotiable quality, authority, legal and safety constraints determine which alternatives
 are feasible before economic ranking begins.
 For a feasible alternative j, define expected net value as the probability-weighted discounted benefits
 minus the attributable costs and losses over time. One possible accounting convention is:


   V(j) = sum over scenarios p(s) × sum over periods [B(j,s,t) - C(j,s,t) - L(j,s,t)] / (1 +
   r)^t

 Here B is the attributable business contribution, C is the full incremental lifecycle and transition
 expenditure, L is other attributable loss from failure or restricted opportunities not already represented
 in B or C, and r is a discount rate consistent with the chosen period. If benefit is already modeled
 relative to the best alternative, do not subtract the same forgone benefit again. Use explicit scenarios
 to represent risk and test whether the ranking changes with assumptions.

    Economic dimension                      How to represent it

    Lifecycle                               Design, build, operation, assurance, adaptation, retirement

    Uncertain adoption                      Scenario-specific demand, outcomes and salvage value

    Transitions                             Forward and reverse expenditures and operational disruption

    Business time                           Actual timing of contributions, lost windows and resources

    Flexibility                             Value of feasible alternatives foregone, future change cost

    Dynamic economics                       Expected ranges of model, hardware and development costs

    Modularity and information              Shared costs, reuse, derivation and refresh costs

 The objective is to choose a feasible alternative with the best expected value, subject to risk appetite,
 commitments and resource constraints. Where rare severe losses are unacceptable, expected value
 alone is not sufficient. Stress tests, exposure limits and mandatory controls are needed. Quantifying
 flexibility or opportunity cost precisely may not be possible; use ranges, documented judgments and
 qualitative constraints instead of fabricated precision.

 The value of deferring a commitment can be assessed by comparing staged strategies, not only static
 implementations. A strategy may serve demand now and reserve the right to specialize later. That
 option is valuable only if the initial execution is adequate and the later transition remains feasible.

 24 | AI MATURITY AND INFORMED SELECTION



 The decision-maker's blind spots
 Economic selection can only be as broad as the alternatives the decision-maker recognizes. A
 transformation consultant may apply rigorous methods, identify bottlenecks and build a credible
 business case while overlooking the possibility of agent-first operations. The conclusion can be logically
 consistent within a software-centric frame, yet not economically optimal across the wider solution
 space.

    Level                                    Primary operating paradigm

    1. Traditional software                  Humans use software to perform work

    2. AI-enabled software                   AI improves the software humans use

    3. Agentic software                      Agents act within software-defined structures

    4. Agent-first operations                Agents perform work and use tools as needed

    5. AI-native operations                  Agents can select, compose, create or retire implementations under authority

    N. Further levels                        Unknown or not yet internalized operating paradigms



      Expanded visibility ≠ required implementation




                                                                                                                  ?


                 L1                     L2                  L3                L4                 L5               N

 Figure 19. Conceptual maturity spectrum. Broader understanding can reveal more alternatives without making the most
 advanced implementation the default choice.

 Maturity bias is the systematic limitation of one's perceived solution space by current understanding
 and experience. Distinguish understanding maturity, experiential maturity and implementation
 maturity. A decision-maker can understand and have experienced agent-first operations while
 deliberately selecting a conventional deterministic service because it is safer, cheaper or more reliable.
 The same service chosen by someone unaware of alternatives may look identical but reflects a different
 quality of decision.

 Agents building applications for humans does not itself establish a higher operating paradigm. Nor does
 engineering an agent as a permanent, narrowly scoped application necessarily exploit the ability to
 instantiate and discard workers dynamically while retaining durable knowledge, skills and records.

 This is a conceptual classification, not a validated assessment scale. There may be further levels that the
 most experienced practitioners cannot yet envision. Expertise in a discipline does not protect its
 practitioners from obsolete premises. Seek experiential evidence, test unfamiliar alternatives and
 remain prepared to revise the frame itself.

 25 | PRACTICAL SELECTION PROTOCOL



 From requirement to a governed decision
 The following protocol translates Liquid Software into a repeatable operating discipline for a capability
 owner, agent coordinator or architect. It deliberately allows a no-agent or no-new-software conclusion.
 Apply it at a meaningful level of scope and within the entity's authority model.

    Step                                    Decision and evidence

    1. Define need                          Specify the outcome, CRD, quality, latency, authority, evidence and acceptance
                                            conditions.

    2. Check alternatives                   Find existing functions, skills, services, models, human routes and the option of
                                            doing nothing.

    3. Fulfill and observe                  Where safe, meet the need now; record usage, costs, exceptions, delays and
                                            actual value.

    4. Compare                              Estimate lifecycle costs, demand scenarios, time to value, transitions, flexibility
                                            and reuse.

    5. Select granularity                   Specialize only the parts that warrant it; compare local, hosted and deterministic
                                            mechanisms.

    6. Validate and authorize               Test equivalent outcomes, controls, dependencies, failure modes, permissions
                                            and rollback.

    7. Operate and revisit                  Observe actual results; trigger review when economics, requirements or
                                            technology change.

    8. Adapt or retire                      Preserve source information, commitments, accepted meaning, and necessary
                                            fallback.

 An example of a lightweight decision record includes the capability identifier and version, owner,
 required outcome, baseline path, alternatives considered, assumptions about demand and quality, fixed
 and marginal costs, evidence, selected realization, authority, review triggers and rollback plan. This is a
 record of a decision, not a permanent constraint on future decisions. Its assumptions remain visible and
 revisable.
 A recurring review might identify high inference expenditure for an already well-understood
 calculation, a high-maintenance application used rarely, or a cache no longer delivering savings.
 Prioritize candidates by potential net benefit and risk rather than by number of invocations alone. The
 optimizer may propose changes; the authorization to deploy them is a separate matter.

 A critical test is whether the process can honestly choose existing software, no new software, a
 deterministic function, a cheaper model or direct intelligent execution. If its incentives always lead to
 building, its economic neutrality has already been compromised.

 27 | GOVERNANCE AND OPERATIONAL SAFEGUARDS



 What should not become liquid
 Information ownership, legal responsibilities and accepted commitments cannot be treated as
 disposable implementation details. An organization must preserve the evidence and semantic
 continuity required to understand past actions and govern future ones. Human decisions define the
 scope of delegated freedom; a cheaper execution route cannot grant itself new authority.

 For any candidate change, evaluate whether it preserves required inputs and outputs, privacy and
 access restrictions, quality criteria, decision rights, traceability, availability, error handling and
 escalation. For critical functions, use representative and adversarial tests, independent validation where
 appropriate, controlled release, monitoring and a reliable recovery path. A new component should not
 be promoted merely because it was easy to generate or passed a few examples.

 The principle of degrees of decision freedom in the broader Liquid Software work distinguishes
 higher-level constraints from decisions that actors may legitimately make within them [9]. Deterministic
 guardrails can support flexibility by clearly defining what must remain true while leaving other
 execution choices open. This also applies to an optimization agent: its permission to propose changes is
 not automatically permission to deploy code, transfer data, make purchases or withdraw services.
 There are cases where dedicated implementations are appropriate before actual demand is observed:
 safety-critical controls, contractual service commitments, regulatory reporting, hard real-time
 requirements, privacy-preserving infrastructure or predictable high-volume workloads. A viable
 implementation may also require stable human-facing interfaces where accessibility, training and
 organizational coordination matter. These are not counterexamples to Liquid Software; they are
 instances where full requirements justify specialization.

 Measure actual change costs and maintain stability when change brings no material benefit. The
 architecture must support both reversible experimentation and durable, predictable operations. The
 objective is controlled economic adaptability, not unrestricted mutation.

 28 | RESEARCH AGENDA



 What remains to be demonstrated
 This paper proposes an economic and architectural framework, not an empirically validated claim that
 Liquid Software improves every organization. The evidence on failed projects, feature adoption and
 inference pricing demonstrates problems and changes in the available technology. It does not measure
 the causal effect of adopting the complete proposed approach.

 A meaningful evaluation would compare matched capabilities across direct agentic, hybrid and
 conventional software paths. It would measure successful completion against shared acceptance
 criteria, full lifecycle costs, setup and transition burden, realized business value, flexibility in response to
 change, and the cost of errors. Tracking the same capabilities over time would show when the initially
 preferred realization ceases to be optimal.

    Research question                       Potential measurement

    Does delayed commitment                 Avoided builds, abandoned candidates, realized adoption and salvage
    reduce waste?

    Does earlier execution create           Time to first outcome, cumulative contribution, missed opportunities
    value?

    How much does modularity                Cross-activity reuse, change effort, interface overhead and dependency risk
    help?

    Where do local models win?              Cost per successful execution, utilization, reliability and operational burden

    How should data be derived?             Retrieval demand, latency, refresh cost, correctness and unused artifacts

    What is the value of                    Response to novel needs, workaround costs, time and value of policy changes
    flexibility?

 A further research question concerns the maturity of decision-makers: how can we distinguish
 deliberate specialization from a limited conception of available alternatives? Comparative design
 exercises could examine which solution classes practitioners recognize before and after hands-on
 agent-first experience. The proposed maturity spectrum should be refined through evidence rather
 than treated as a definitive ranking of individuals.

 The numerical examples and charts in this paper are explanatory models. They are deliberately simple
 and should be replaced with actual entity-level data in any business case. Further work should examine
 boundary conditions, measurement methods, security and governance in real operational
 environments, and the impact of rapidly evolving model and software costs.

    materialization

 The fundamental change is not merely cheaper software. It is the ability to separate the fulfillment of a
 need from the creation of dedicated infrastructure. General-purpose intelligence can use existing tools,
 learn from actual execution and specialize selectively. Modularity makes that specialization
 independently replaceable. Durable information and human decisions preserve continuity while
 realizations change.

 The economic objective includes the software never built, the time not lost waiting, the failed
 transitions avoided, the options left open and the ability to benefit from changing execution
 technologies. It equally includes the benefits of predictable, efficient and stable software where those
 benefits justify its existence.


   Information provides continuity. Human decisions define the boundaries.
   Intelligence fulfills needs. Software emerges as an optimization.

 The highest maturity is not the greatest use of agents or the least use of software. It is the ability to
 make informed, continuously revisable decisions about the most appropriate form of execution, while
 acknowledging the limits of one's understanding.
