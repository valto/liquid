01 / THESIS



Liquid Software:
The Economics of Execution
A dynamic framework for when intelligence should act directly, when software should
materialize, and when existing implementations should evolve or dissolve.

Conceptual source: Valto Loikkanen | Version 2.0 | November 27, 2026


Abstract
AI changes not only the cost of producing software, but also the need to produce it. Work can be
performed directly by agents, through reusable skills and specialized components, through
deterministic software, or through a combination of these. Liquid Software makes the choice between
these realizations a continuous economic and architectural decision.

The objective is to maximize risk-adjusted cumulative net value over time, including time to value, total
lifecycle costs, foregone opportunities, the cost of failed adoption and transitions, and the flexibility
lost when behavior is fixed. Modularity extends this principle to models, capabilities, information
processing and infrastructure.


   Intelligence is the default capability. Software is an optimization.


From market-level to entity-level economics
SaaS has made even narrow applications viable by spreading design, development and maintenance
across many users and organizations. When an individual or organization can fulfill needs directly with
agents and create software on demand, a dedicated implementation must justify its value within that
entity. External SaaS, shared components and existing infrastructure remain legitimate alternatives.

Lower software development cost alone does not justify more software: the cost and capability of
direct agentic execution are changing at the same time. The question is what should exist, in what form,
at what granularity, and for how long.


   You don’t build a house for one night visit or road to walk by once.
   Valto Loikkanen, November 2026

02 / OPERATING PARADIGM



Intelligence can use any tool
Consider a self-driving car and a sufficiently capable general-purpose humanoid. The self-driving car is
an advanced, intelligence-empowered vehicle. The humanoid could instead drive a decades-old car,
operate a truck, ride a bicycle, make food and perform digital chores concurrently where safe and
feasible. The intelligence is not permanently bound to a single tool. This is a conceptual analogy, not a
claim about today’s humanoid capabilities.

  Intelligence embedded in a tool                               Intelligence that can use tools

  Self-driving car                                              Humanoid driving a compatible existing vehicle

  Specialized industrial robot                                  General-purpose worker using specialized machinery

  AI-enabled application                                        Agent using existing software, APIs and tools

  Software-defined agent workflow                               Agent choosing and composing capabilities as needed


A high-utilization autonomous taxi fleet or a factory robot can still justify specialization. The difference
is that specialized infrastructure is chosen for demonstrated advantages rather than treated as the
prerequisite for intelligent work.

The execution continuum

    Direct agentic               Reusable             Deterministic               Software                Dedicated
      execution                    skills              functions                  modules                 software



Figure 1. Different steps of an activity may occupy different points. The continuum is reversible, not a mandatory maturity
progression.

Software is also a constraint
A road accelerates travel along a chosen route, but can become a barrier when it has too few exits.
Software encodes assumptions about workflows, data, interaction and rules; these may restrict both
humans and agents. Premature determinism fixes behavior before variability and exceptions are
understood. Expose functions independently, allow alternative paths, and retain agentic fallback where
permitted.


   Materialize determinism only where its benefits exceed its lifecycle
   costs and the value of the flexibility it removes.

03 / ECONOMIC DECISION MODEL



Beyond runtime cost
Compare all candidate realizations against the same required outcome, quality, latency, reliability,
safety and authority boundaries. Account for the full lifecycle, including the cost of enabling
intelligence itself. Do not equate lower inference cost or lower coding cost with superior economics.

  Lifecycle dimension         Agentic realization                              Software realization

  Design                      Purpose, constraints, context, acceptance        Requirements, architecture, interface
                              criteria                                         design

  Build & deploy              Skills, permissions, tools, runtime setup        Development, integration, testing, rollout

  Execution                   Inference, retries, tool use, time,              Compute, infrastructure, operating time
                              supervision

  Assurance                   Evaluation, uncertainty, verification,           Tests, monitoring, validation, controls
                              controls

  Maintenance                 Context, skills, models, runtime upkeep          Code, dependencies, security,
                                                                               integrations

  Adaptation                  Changes to goals, instructions or tools          Changes to code, data models and
                                                                               workflows

  Retirement                  Remove or replace realization; preserve          Decommission, migration, restoration
                              records


Optimize net value, not just cost
For each alternative and plausible demand scenario, estimate the discounted value delivered over the
relevant horizon, subtract full lifecycle expenditure, transition costs and losses attributable to rigidity or
failure. Compare against the best feasible alternative, including doing nothing. Account for foregone
business opportunities without counting the same loss twice.


   Decision objective: maximize expected cumulative net business value
   over time, subject to required quality, risk, performance and
   governance constraints.

A low-cost execution path can be economically inferior if it delays revenue or blocks a better outcome. A fixed
implementation can be justified before repeated use where safety, legal obligations, predictable latency or reliability require
it.

04 / EVIDENCE AND INVESTMENT RISK



The cost of software that fails to create
value
Unused, underused and unsuccessful implementations are different forms of economic exposure.
Evidence from both public audits and product analytics demonstrates the problem, although there is no
reliable universal failure rate for all software.

  Evidence                                   What it establishes

  80% of features rarely or never used       Pendo’s 2019 product usage study reported this share for the average
                                             product in its data. It estimated up to $29.5B in cloud software R&D
                                             associated with these features. [1]

  4 of 6 completed projects missed or        GAO’s July 2026 review of eligible US Technology Modernization Fund
  were off track for savings                 projects, measured as of June 2025. Seven of the 68 reviewed projects
                                             had been cancelled and no longer expected savings. [2]

  $1B already spent before cancellation      GAO reported that the US Air Force cancelled ECSS after around $1B
                                             had been expended, with another $1B expected and limited projected
                                             capability. [3]

  $34M+ to change and change back            The Washington Post reported this cost for Washington, D.C.’s
                                             migration to and return from a replacement payroll system. [5]


These are different populations and measures, not comparable failure rates. Pendo is vendor research; GAO provides
official public-sector audit findings. The D.C. figure is a reported historical case, not an audit estimate.

Double transition cost
Introducing an unsuccessful human-facing system can cause disruption twice: first through training,
migration and process redesign; then through retraining, data reconciliation and restoration. UK
government research in 2023 reported a few such reversals when replacement supply-chain systems
proved worse, unusable or otherwise unsuitable. [4]

The damage can extend beyond expenditure to customer loss, employee frustration, deferred
initiatives and diminished confidence. These consequences are distinct from the original build cost and
should be included when comparing alternatives.


   The greatest economic benefit may be the software that never needs
   to be created.

05 / TIME AND CHANGING ECONOMICS



Value before infrastructure
Direct agentic execution, where feasible, lets a need be fulfilled before a dedicated implementation
exists. Actual execution then exposes frequency, exceptions, cost, requirements and reuse
opportunities. The organization can invest in software after evidence emerges rather than wait for a
speculative build.

Conventional software-first sequence

         Design                           Build                       Deploy                          Value

Agent-first sequence, where feasible

                                            Business value through execution


                        Function A                         Module B                         Function C

Figure 2. Illustrative time-to-value comparison; software development can occur alongside ongoing work. Agentic
execution also has setup time, and some tasks require upfront software.

Opportunity cost includes the value of delayed outcomes, lost customers or market windows, and
scarce management, employee or capital resources diverted from other work. Deferring a permanent
investment also preserves the option to change direction as evidence improves. Even identical eventual
software can produce very different cumulative business value.

The economic threshold moves
Stanford’s 2025 AI Index reports that the inference price for a model at approximately GPT-3.5-level
MMLU performance fell from $20 to $0.07 per million tokens between November 2022 and October
2024, over 280-fold. [6] This is a benchmark-specific historical comparison, not a measure of
whole-agent costs or a guarantee of future declines.

Local models and owned hardware may lower marginal cost compared with retail API pricing,
particularly at high utilization. Compare full hardware amortization, electricity, operations, model
quality, latency, retries and supervision. Software production costs are also falling, so reassess both
sides, not only inference.

A simple break-even model: if fixed incremental software cost is F, agentic cost per successful execution is a and software
cost is s, the threshold is F/(a−s) when a exceeds s. Actual decisions also require time, maintenance, quality, demand
uncertainty, flexibility and opportunity cost.

06 / MODULARITY



Optimize every capability, not every
application
Modularity is the central architectural enabler. Optimize at the smallest economically meaningful unit,
not at the level of an entire application or agent. A component should be independently reusable and
replaceable while retaining a coherent purpose, interaction contract and assurance requirements.

The CRD as the stable semantic layer
Valto Loikkanen’s Capability Requirements Document (CRD) framework distinguishes a Capability MLE
(Minimum Logical Element), its Interaction Contract MLEs, and one or more realizations. A CRD
specifies a complete contextually meaningful ability independently of the current implementation. [7]
Individual contracts or internal operations can be assigned different realization mechanisms.

Capability MLE / CRD
Purpose, requirements, boundaries, interaction contracts, evidence and unknowns


      Advanced                  Small local             Deterministic               Software                 Existing
   reasoning model               model                 reasoning layer              function               service / API



Figure 3. Illustrative realization options. They are composable alternatives, not a ranking or a fixed sequence.

Use the least costly realization that demonstrably meets the capability’s requirements. An advanced
model may interpret novel cases; a smaller local model may classify; a deterministic rule may validate;
existing APIs may execute authorized actions. Route uncertain cases to higher-capability models or
humans as appropriate.

MDIL/Kide illustrates another emerging option: the provider describes declared deterministic reasoning
separated from language generation, with traceable decisions. [8] Such components may reduce
repeated probabilistic reasoning for suitable tasks. Their actual economics, suitability and availability
must be evaluated rather than assumed.

Modularity across the stack
Compose agents, skills, models, contracts, functions, data processing and infrastructure independently.
Preserve durable context and records outside replaceable runtimes. Agents may be instantiated for a
task and dismissed without losing organizational knowledge. More modularity increases reuse and
off-ramps, but coordination, testing and interface costs still matter.

07 / INFORMATION ECONOMICS



Process information for a purpose
The same reasoning applies to information. Data availability is not in itself sufficient justification for
processing it. Extraction, classification, embedding, summarization and relationship construction
consume resources, can become stale, and may never contribute to a retrieved answer or action.


   Decide what processing is needed for a purpose. Defer additional
   processing unless retrieval or an established requirement justifies it.


    Retain source              Process on             Cache or retain              Selective                Extensive
     information                retrieval              useful results            preprocessing            preprocessing



Figure 4. Processing can move in either direction. Raw information still needs sufficient ingestion, security and
discoverability.

A demand-driven processing model
  Approach                              When it may be justified

  Preserve original sources             Maintain evidence, provenance, access controls and necessary metadata;
                                        comply with retention and deletion obligations.

  Process on retrieval                  A novel or infrequent need can tolerate on-demand computation. Use models
                                        or deterministic extractors matched to the task.

  Cache derived results                 Repeat retrievals justify reuse; retain provenance, freshness and invalidation
                                        rules.

  Preprocess selectively                Known repeated demand, latency, security, discoverability or regulatory
                                        requirements justify upfront investment.

  Re-evaluate and retire                Discard or recompute derived representations when use declines,
                                        requirements change or source data changes.


A useful comparison is the expected cost and value of preprocessing, storage and refresh versus
on-demand retrieval, including quality, latency and the likelihood of use. Keep canonical information
distinct from derived artifacts; avoid irreversible transformation and preserve necessary discoverability.
Some preprocessing, including protective controls, may be non-optional.

08 / DECISION PROTOCOL



Continuous capability economics
Liquid Software is not a one-time technology selection exercise. It is an operating discipline in which
agents and accountable humans repeatedly evaluate the realization of capabilities. The appropriate
implementation may be fully deterministic, fully agentic or hybrid.

  Decision step                          Practical question

  1. Specify the capability              What outcome, constraints and interaction contracts must remain true?

  2. Check existing resources            Can an existing service, module, skill or model fulfill this need?

  3. Execute and observe                 Where safe, meet the need now; record usage, costs, exceptions, quality
                                         and time to value.

  4. Compare alternatives                What is the expected net value over time, including risk, transition costs,
                                         reuse, rigidity and alternative uses of resources?

  5. Materialize selectively             What part merits deterministic realization? Can interfaces preserve
                                         off-ramps and fallback?

  6. Govern and revisit                  What permissions, tests, audit records, monitoring and review triggers are
                                         required?

  7. Evolve or dissolve                  Does this implementation still justify its existence? Preserve necessary
                                         information and controls while replacing or retiring it.


Informed selection and maturity bias
A decision-maker should understand and ideally have experienced the full available spectrum before
recommending an operating model. A lower-level implementation may be the informed optimum;
selecting it because more flexible alternatives were invisible is a different matter. Unknown alternatives
may remain even at the highest recognized maturity level.

This connects Liquid Software to the companion whitepaper, AI Maturity, Maturity Bias and Informed
Selection. The measure of maturity is not the absence of software, but the ability to continuously
optimize its existence and the realization of capabilities while respecting human-defined authority.


   Instead of making every tool intelligent, give intelligence access to
   every appropriate tool. Specialize only where the economics and
   requirements justify it.
