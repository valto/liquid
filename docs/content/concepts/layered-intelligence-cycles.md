# Layered Intelligence Cycles

**Definition.** Organize reviews around distinct purposes and depths, using suitable recurring cadences and evidence-triggered exceptions while preserving their decision boundaries.

## Abstract

Faster information processing does not remove the need for coordination, accountability or deep deliberation. This concept paper proposes independent choices for what a review examines, how thoroughly it reasons and when it runs. It connects immediate signals with operational and strategic understanding without requiring every layer to be a summary of the one beneath it. The proposal is developed from chapters 2–3 of the supplied Continuous Intelligence companion.

## Purpose comes before the calendar

A daily check might identify delivery exceptions. A weekly review might examine recurring bottlenecks. A strategic review might reconsider the assumptions behind an offering. These are different questions, rather than merely different amounts of the same report.

Cadence and depth are independent. A material market signal can justify an immediate strategic review; a stable operational issue can need a monthly assessment. Continuous, daily, weekly, monthly, quarterly and annual rhythms are illustrative defaults. The framework supplies no universal schedule.

| Review dimension | Question it answers |
| --- | --- |
| Purpose and scope | What decision or uncertainty does this review serve? |
| Evidence and depth | Which sources, alternatives and assumptions need examination? |
| Cadence and triggers | When is examination useful, and what warrants an exception? |
| Authority | What may this review decide, recommend or escalate? |
| Output and revisit conditions | What is retained, and what would make it stale? |

These dimensions form a review charter. A charter can be implemented in ordinary documents and existing management processes. It is a logical description, not a mandatory database schema or new meeting hierarchy.

## Persist understanding with its dependencies

A useful review retains evidence, propositions, beliefs or assessments, and decisions in distinguishable forms. Record the support, confidence, assumptions, applicable context and last review. An assessment that a supplier is dependable should expose the observations and conditions behind it; a new incident can invalidate part of that assessment without erasing its history.

Later reviews can reuse this understanding where it still applies. Reuse reduces repeated reconstruction while keeping correction possible. A retained belief is not a timeless fact. Nor does storing a derived assessment beside an original make the assessment authoritative.

Different reviews can consult shared evidence directly. A strategic assessment need not receive only a filtered weekly summary; an operational reviewer need not inherit every strategic interpretation. Shared provenance helps prevent duplicate reports from being mistaken for independent corroboration.

## Illustrative situation

A business has a quarterly market review and a weekly delivery review. Several customers begin requesting a new service model while delivery performance remains healthy. The signal triggers an earlier market review. Reviewers examine demand, competitors, capacity and competing explanations, then recommend a bounded offer test.

The trigger authorizes examination within the review's scope. It does not by itself authorize a new commercial commitment or deployment. The weekly operating process can continue while the strategic alternative is considered. The example is a design scenario, not evidence of an implemented service.

## Evaluation and limits

Compare a proposed review arrangement against the actual existing process on missed signals, false triggers, decision lead time, review cost and the usefulness of retained reasoning. Include situations where a scheduled review detects something a trigger missed. More reviews can consume attention and worsen coordination.

Review outputs should be useful enough to support decisions, rather than merely countable documents. Test whether a later reviewer can identify an invalidated assumption and determine which conclusions it affects. The appropriate depth depends on impact, uncertainty and reversibility; the paper does not validate a universal depth score.
