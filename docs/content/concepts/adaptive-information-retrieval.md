# Adaptive Information Retrieval

**Definition.** Interpret emerging questions and evolve retrieval structures around observed needs while preserving source evidence and agreed meaning.

Retrieval can combine precise queries, indexes, graphs, semantic search and intelligent interpretation. The system chooses an appropriate path for the current question and evidence.

Repetition may justify a more efficient derived structure. The index remains distinct from the information and accepted assertions it serves. Better retrieval cannot recreate source evidence that was never retained.

## Continuity across methods

Changing the retrieval method should preserve purpose, permission, freshness, provenance and uncertainty. A response derived from several sources must make its evidential limits clear.

## Invest in processing around demand

Preserving appropriate source material does not require preprocessing every possible interpretation. Processing on retrieval, caching, selective preprocessing and broader preprocessing are reversible choices. Compare their value and complete costs, including discovery, minimum metadata, authorization, freshness, invalidation and rebuilding.

Some preparation is necessary before a source can be found or accessed safely. Within those constraints, observed need can justify a specific derived structure. Retiring that structure does not authorize deleting source evidence or pending unaccepted work, and its location does not confer canonical authority.

## An illustrative retrieval investment

The supplied Economics of Execution v3, page 23, assumes €1,300 for full extraction and horizon upkeep, then €0.01 per relevant-document retrieval, against €0.20 for on-demand extraction. At 1,000 retrievals the costs are €1,310 and €200; at 10,000 they are €1,400 and €2,000. Equal cost occurs at 6,842.105… retrievals, so the first whole count favoring preprocessing is 6,843. These are explanatory assumptions, not measured retrieval prices or demonstrated performance.

Basic indexing and access controls are already available equally to both paths. Retrieval events are distinct from archive size and unique-document count; repeat demand can instead justify selective caching. Necessary discovery, freshness, accessibility and protective controls remain costs and requirements of either approach. The original source's rounded approximately 6,842 wording is preserved in its PDF and transcription.

## Validate the change

Compare correctness, total cost, latency and recovery across existing, intelligent and hybrid paths. Include exact readback, missing evidence, changed source revisions and retirement of derived optimizations.

## Reuse understanding with its basis

Persisting evidence, propositions, assessments and decisions can reduce repeated reconstruction. Keep their assumptions, dependencies, confidence, scope and last review so a changed source or condition can invalidate the affected interpretation. Retained understanding remains revisable; a summary does not become an unquestionable fact.

The [Continuous Intelligence companion](https://valto.github.io/liquid/whitepapers/continuous-intelligence/) cites Optivian's maintained deal-context example. Its reported token reduction illustrates a possible preprocessing benefit, rather than independently establishing universal superiority. Preparation, updates, coverage, raw-source access and accepted answer quality belong in the comparison alongside query costs.
