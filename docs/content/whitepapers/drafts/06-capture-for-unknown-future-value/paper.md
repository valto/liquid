# Capture for Unknown Future Value

*Preserving evidence beyond the questions we can anticipate today*


**Conceptual source and direction: Valto Loikkanen.** Research and drafting: Codex (OpenAI), at his request. Research draft 1.0 · 26 November 2026.

## Abstract

Valto Loikkanen argues that information should retain authorized richness beyond the retrieval needs anticipated at capture. A record reduced to predefined fields can lose evidence that later questions require. This paper develops a source-preserving architecture in which originals coexist with derived interpretations and efficient retrieval structures. It uses his example of birds behind a person during a walk to examine retrospective questions, while distinguishing capture from complete knowledge. Preservation is selective and governed; it does not imply recording everything or preferring unstructured storage for every kind of information. [S01]

## 1. The question that arrives later

During a walk, a person records an environment for one reason. Later, they ask what the birds behind them were doing. A stored route and a few manually selected observations may not answer the question. A suitably oriented recording might retain relevant evidence even though bird behavior was not the original purpose.

The example illustrates an asymmetry. A new presentation can often be generated from retained evidence. Evidence omitted at capture may be impossible to recover. Decisions about preservation therefore affect which future questions remain answerable.

This does not mean a recording answers every question. A bird may be hidden, outside the captured field or insufficiently resolved. Observable behavior can support a description without establishing a definitive health assessment. The system should state what the evidence supports and what it does not.

## 2. Preserve optionality within deliberate boundaries

**Capture for Unknown Future Value** means preserving enough attributable original information to support meaningful future interpretation beyond today's selected fields, within authorized purpose, retention and resource boundaries.

The objective is useful optionality. Richness is valuable when it preserves evidence that may matter; unlimited accumulation is not automatically valuable. Capture can also burden storage, energy, security, understanding and other people's interests.

A deliberate retention choice differs from accidental loss caused by a narrow application model. The proposed architecture makes that choice explicit: what is retained, why, under whose authority and with what foreseeable limits?

## 3. Separate original information from interpretation

| Layer | Example | Treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Original | A recorded conversation or authored document | Preserve identity and integrity under retention rules |
| Context | Capture time, source, relevant settings and rights | Retain sufficient attribution and interpretive context |
| Assertion | A claim extracted from the evidence | Record evidence, method and uncertainty |
| Retrieval representation | Search index or structured observation table | Rebuild or replace when useful |
| Presentation | Briefing, timeline or chart | Adapt to the current recipient and purpose |

An assertion can later become an accepted decision or record through a declared process. It should not become authoritative merely because a model produced it. Conversely, a structured original remains an original: an accepted transaction is not necessarily a derived interpretation of some richer recording.

PIOS and EIOS distinguish original information, knowledge and use. The present proposal extends that distinction to preservation decisions made before future questions are known. [S07, S08]

## 4. Why multimodal preservation matters

Text, sound, images, spatial observations and other measurements can retain different aspects of an event. In the walking example, combining relevant modalities could preserve context that a single summary omits. No combination captures reality completely: sensors, positions, resolution, timing and interpretation constrain the record.

The contribution is therefore not an ultimate recording format. It is an architecture that preserves available evidence in a form that can be reinterpreted and connected later. Different domains may justify different capture choices.

Preserving source form also supports disagreement. A later interpretation can be checked against the original rather than relying only on an earlier model's conclusion. Improved analysis can produce a new derived result while preserving the history of prior interpretations.

## 5. SQL and the information-loss question

The conversation uses SQL as a strong example of retrieval-first design. The defensible target is the decision to retain only anticipated fields, rather than the relational model itself. Codd's relational model explicitly pursued independence between information and its internal representation. [S18]

A relational database can retain structured originals, event records, rich metadata or references to multimodal objects. An object store can also lose meaning if it preserves bytes without attribution or usable context.

The important questions are what information survives, which transformations are irreversible, and whether later interpretation can return to the source. Storage technology should follow those requirements rather than substitute for them.

## 6. Future interpretation can increase usefulness

Retained originals may support better extraction, new questions or corrected interpretations. This is a conditional possibility, not an assured increase in economic value. Information can become irrelevant, inaccessible, misleading or too costly to use.

An architecture can nevertheless preserve the option to revisit it. A new bird-recognition method could propose different observations from the same recording. Each result should retain the source interval, method version and uncertainty, allowing comparison with earlier results.

The future-value hypothesis should be tested through actual later questions, rather than justified solely by the amount of data stored.

## 7. Governance is part of preservation

Capture must respect the relevant participants and authority. Owning a device does not automatically make every observed person or environment unrestricted personal information. This paper offers an architectural principle, not legal advice or permission for surveillance.

Retention, access and derived inference require separate consideration. Permission to preserve a source for one purpose should not be assumed to authorize every later analysis or disclosure. Revoking access can prevent future use without retrieving copies already released.

Deletion and retention should therefore be deliberate lifecycle decisions. The phrase original does not imply eternal retention, and the phrase derived does not imply that a copy is harmless or freely shareable.

## 8. A preservation experiment

Select an authorized source set and compare two representations: a source-preserving package and a reduced field-only package. Define questions at capture, then introduce independently selected questions later.

Measure answerability, evidence quality, interpretation cost, storage cost and errors. Include cases where richer capture provides no advantage or introduces misleading evidence. Verify that the retained package can be exported and reconstructed independently of its original application.

The strongest result would demonstrate useful new questions answered from retained sources at an acceptable total cost. A larger archive alone is not success.

## 9. Relationship to the wider architecture

Preservation creates future interpretive possibilities. Adaptive retrieval makes selected possibilities efficient. The Adaptive Interface presents the resulting information for a current need. Liquid Software changes the implementations that serve it.

Loikkanen's distinctive connected principle is to avoid allowing the temporary means of retrieval and presentation to determine all the information that will survive. It is a design priority that can be adopted incrementally, alongside structured systems and explicit retention choices.
