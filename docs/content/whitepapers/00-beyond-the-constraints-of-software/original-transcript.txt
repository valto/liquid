 Beyond the Constraints
 of Software
 The transition toward an information-centric,
 intelligence-native world

 Conceptual source: Valto Loikkanen
 26 November 2026 | Version 1.0 | Conceptual synthesis



 Abstract
 The digital world has been organized around the limitations of software. Information is reduced to
 predefined storage structures, expressed through fixed presentation formats and made accessible
 through predetermined interfaces. Human activities and economic relationships have in turn
 adapted to what software can support. Artificial intelligence introduces the possibility of reversing
 these dependencies: information can be preserved in its original richness, interpreted when needed
 and served through interfaces and implementations that adapt to actual usage.

 This paper presents an umbrella thesis connecting Liquid Software, adaptive interfaces, capture for
 unknown future value, adaptive retrieval, individual and entity information systems, and degrees of
 decision freedom. Intelligence provides a general capability to respond to needs; software becomes
 an increasingly fluid optimization. Relatively durable information, shared semantics, capability
 requirements and human-defined boundaries maintain coherence and continuity.


    Information provides continuity. Human decisions define the
    boundaries. Intelligence fulfills needs, with software emerging as an
    optimization.

 This paper is a synthesis of concepts articulated by Valto Loikkanen in the source discussion. It is a proposed paradigm
 and reference map, not a claim that the technologies are fully realized or that every underlying idea is new.

 The historical bottleneck
 Software made the digital economy possible, but the effort required to build, distribute and
 maintain it also made it a structural constraint. To make computation reliable and affordable,
 developers specified in advance what information would be captured, which operations would be
 available and how users would interact with them. Scale rewarded standardization, and users and
 organizations adapted to the resulting applications.

 This is more than a development productivity problem. The limitations of software influence what
 can be recorded, which relationships can be expressed, what services are economically viable and
 how work is organized. A system designed for anticipated needs often becomes an obstacle to
 needs that emerge later.

 Intelligence first, software second
 The proposed reversal is to establish general intelligent capability before deciding where dedicated
 software is needed. An intelligent system can interpret a new request and attempt to fulfill it
 directly. Repetition and operational requirements then justify deterministic code, caching, indexes
 or other optimizations. This does not eliminate software: it changes why software exists, when it is
 produced and how long it remains useful.

 The thesis is a direction of travel, not a prediction that every domain will become fluid.
 Safety-critical controls, contractual guarantees, real-time systems and other deterministic
 requirements can justify stable implementations from the outset. The point is to avoid unnecessary
 rigidity, not to maximize change.


    We have organized the world around what software could do.
    Increasingly, we can organize software around what the world
    actually needs.

 Information before structure
 Storage is the deepest of the historical constraints because it determines which evidence survives.
 A report, interface or application can be regenerated; discarded observations may be impossible to
 reconstruct. When a conversation is reduced to the fields of a CRM system, information that was
 not anticipated by the schema is often lost.

 A capture-first architecture preserves authorized original information in the richest practical form:
 text, documents, images, audio, video, spatial recordings and sensor observations. A hypothetical
 near-maximal capture of an experience might include synchronized 3D video, spatial audio, thermal
 imagery, LiDAR and environmental measurements. No set of sensors records all reality, but richer
 capture expands the questions that can be asked later.

 The birds behind the observer
 Suppose a person walks through a park without noticing birds behind them. Years later they ask
 what the birds were doing and whether they appeared healthy. Spatial and multimodal recordings
 could permit retrospective analysis of behavior and visible health indicators. A predefined table
 containing only location and walking duration could not answer those questions. Even rich evidence
 may not establish actual health; the system must distinguish observations, interpretations and
 unknowns.

   Source evidence                   Preserve original recordings, timestamps, provenance and relevant context.

   Interpretation                    Extract observations, relationships, propositions and uncertainty as needs
                                     emerge.

   Derived structures                Generate SQL tables, spatial indexes, graphs, vector indexes or other
                                     retrieval representations.

   Reprocessing                      Revisit the original evidence with improved models or newly relevant
                                     questions.


 SQL is valuable as an efficient retrieval and transactional technology, not inherently inferior as a
 storage technology. The architectural error is allowing a narrowly anticipated retrieval schema to
 determine the full extent of what is preserved. Authoritative structured facts may themselves be
 canonical when they originate as structured records. Derived representations should not silently
 replace richer original evidence.


    Capture without needing to know what will be asked. Structure
    according to what is asked. Optimize according to how often it is
    asked.

 Privacy, consent, data minimization, retention, cost and legal obligations govern capture. Maximum future value means
 maximum appropriate richness within those boundaries, not indiscriminate surveillance.

 The interface becomes adaptive
 Documents, slide decks, infographics, dashboards and applications can be understood as different
 interfaces to information. Historically each has required separate production, formatting and
 maintenance. Software also limits information through fixed screens, forms and workflows. In both
 cases the presentation format precedes the information need and constrains its expression.

 UI on the fly reverses this relationship. Information exists independently of a presentation, and an
 interface is generated or selected according to the actual recipient and situation. It may be a
 temporary view, a reusable artifact, an interactive tool or a conversation. A snapshot can still be
 retained when it is itself evidence, a publication or a formal record.

  Dimension               Determines the interaction

  What                    The information or capability needed, its nature and relevant relationships.

  To whom                 Recipient, role, expertise, language, accessibility and authority.

  Where                   Device, physical or operational environment, available attention and context.

  Why                     The purpose: understanding, communicating, deciding, retrieving or acting.

  How                     The resulting presentation, request and response format, derived from the above
                          or explicitly requested.



 A unified human and machine interface
 Humans may express intent in speech, text or actions; agents and applications may use structured
 requests or natural language. The same semantics, permissions and capability contracts can
 underpin both. A machine requester can specify the desired information and output shape using
 GraphQL, REST-style requests or other agreed conventions; a human can describe an equivalent
 need in words.

 The choice of request format should not prescribe the responding implementation. A
 GraphQL-shaped request can be fulfilled agentically, deterministically or through a hybrid path.
 Standard GraphQL still requires a valid schema, and REST does not by itself define a response
 schema. A practical system therefore needs supported request grammars, discoverable or
 negotiated contracts and validation rules. Dynamic semantics must not silently break a declared
 contract.


    What, to whom, where and why determine how. The interface is an
    outcome, not a starting point.

 Liquid Software
 Liquid Software is a proposed paradigm in which software implementations can continuously
 emerge, adapt, solidify and retire according to actual needs, within governed semantic and technical
 boundaries. It brings together the earlier ideas of UI on the fly and backend on the fly, and extends
 the same logic to machine interfaces, integrations, retrieval and execution.

 The central change is not merely the generation of code. The underlying capability may already be
 available through intelligence. Dedicated software becomes useful when it improves speed, cost,
 predictability, repeatability or other operational characteristics. The capability and its contract
 remain relatively independent of the particular implementation.

 A reversible lifecycle
   1. Emergence                A novel or infrequent need is interpreted and fulfilled through intelligence
                               where possible.

   2. Observation              Recurring semantic patterns, costs, latencies, reliability and dependencies are
                               measured.

   3. Solidification           Validated code, indexes, caches, skills or services take over appropriate parts
                               of fulfillment.

   4. Adaptation               Implementations are regenerated, combined or revised as information, needs
                               and requirements evolve.

   5. Retirement               Unused optimizations are removed or archived; valid contracts retain an
                               appropriate fallback.


 This is not a one-way journey from agentic to deterministic. Demand can be seasonal or short-lived.
 A temporary project may create intense demand for a particular view or API, followed by little use.
 If the need returns, the implementation can be reactivated or regenerated. Some needs will always
 be best served through a hybrid of intelligence and stable software.

 Stable foundations make fluidity possible
 Canonical documentation, terminology, capability requirements documents (CRDs), reusable skills,
 design conventions, permissions, business rules and technical standards define what must remain
 coherent. They also guide agents on where to look and how to reason when a particular
 requirement is not predefined. Custom behavior is thereby constrained by shared principles rather
 than left arbitrary.


    Liquid Software is governed fluidity, not software without structure.

 The self-optimizing information system
 A concrete technical realization is an adaptive retrieval and fulfillment layer that can accept
 requests in supported formats without requiring every possible semantic query to be implemented
 in advance. Shared documentation and terminology make the request interpretable. The receiving
 system selects a fulfillment path independently of whether the requester is a human, agent or
 traditional application.

   Request                      Identify the requester, represented principal, purpose and expected response
                                contract.

   Interpret and authorize      Resolve semantics and scope; check permissions and relevant obligations.

   Fulfill                      Route to agentic hybrid retrieval, deterministic software or a combination.

   Validate and respond         Check schema, provenance, freshness, constraints and uncertainty; deliver
                                the agreed representation.

   Observe and improve          Monitor semantic request patterns and operational outcomes; identify
                                candidates for optimization.



 The nightly optimization process
 A monitoring agent can observe daily and weekly usage, group equivalent requests and evaluate
 the cost and benefit of producing more permanent retrieval structures or API implementations. A
 scheduled optimization run can propose creation, updates, consolidation or retirement. Frequency
 is a strong signal; latency targets, correctness, sensitivity, implementation cost and service
 commitments also determine whether an optimization is justified.

 The optimizer must not bypass the authority and semantic checks that apply to agentic retrieval. It
 should test against CRDs and representative queries, verify access controls and freshness, measure
 regressions, deploy under defined approval rules and preserve rollback. Retiring a particular
 implementation must not remove an externally committed capability unless that contract is
 changed appropriately.

 Illustrative example
 An entity frequently asks for a weekly view of open commitments by owner. Initially the system
 interprets its documentation and retrieves evidence across sources. Repetition warrants a derived
 index and validated query implementation. Later, the activity ends and the index is retired if no
 obligations require it. A future valid request can fall back to intelligent retrieval without changing
 the meaning of the requested information.


    The request defines the need and the expected response. Actual
    usage determines the implementation.

 Individuals and entities as anchors
 At the architectural level, the model distinguishes two types of information principal: an individual
 and an entity. PIOS represents the personal information, intelligence and continuity of an individual;
 EIOS represents the corresponding needs of an entity. These are conceptual anchors, not a
 requirement that every technical resource have a single physical endpoint or that legal ownership
 of all information be exclusive.

 The implementation-level requester can still be a human, an agent or an application. It may act
 directly or indirectly on behalf of an individual or entity. An agent can initiate a request to serve a
 delegated objective without a human explicitly formulating that particular request.

  Independent dimension                   Possibilities

  Principal                               Individual (PIOS) or entity (EIOS)

  Operational actor                       Human, agent or application

  Interaction                             Natural language, structured request, protocol or other agreed format

  Fulfillment                             Agentic, deterministic or hybrid


 The immediate requester, represented principal, ultimate beneficiary and purpose should be
 distinguished for authority and accountability. Neither the actor nor its interaction format
 determines the other side's implementation. PIOS and EIOS can exchange information with each
 other through established sharing arrangements, retaining separate control and continuity.

 Complementary architectural concepts
  Concept                                Role in the architecture

  PIOS / EIOS                            Persistent identity, canonical information, knowledge, objectives and
                                         continuity.

  PAIX                                   Framework for establishing contact and coordinating interactions
                                         between parties.

  CRDs                                   Implementation-independent capability requirements and acceptance
                                         boundaries.

  Personal Application Ecosystem         Applications as replaceable mechanisms serving the individual and
                                         their information.

  Documentation and semantics            Shared vocabulary, provenance, governing rules and guidance for
                                         resolving unspecified needs.

 These concepts are complementary rather than mandatory dependencies. A system may adopt adaptive interfaces or
 capture-first storage independently of PIOS, EIOS or PAIX.

 Degrees of decision freedom
 As the rigidity of software decreases, human decision speed may become a major constraint, insofar
 as humans retain ultimate authority. The answer is not to eliminate human control, but to increase
 the leverage of decisions already made. Human sovereignty does not require separate human
 approval of every operational step.

 Decisions are never made in a vacuum. Every decision is made within an existing space of freedom,
 shaped by applicable law, rights, agreements, values, resources, circumstances and prior decisions.
 Higher-level decisions can establish a lower-level decision space without prescribing each decision
 made within it.

   External boundaries         Applicable law, rights, contractual commitments, physical conditions and
                               available resources.

   Principal decisions         Purpose, values, strategic objectives and decisions reserved to the individual
                               or authorized entity governance.

   Delegated freedom           Objectives, responsibilities, budgets, permissions, methods and scope of
                               independent action.

   Operational choices         Contextual decisions and execution within inherited boundaries.

   Escalation                  Conflicting objectives, insufficient authority, exceeded limits or reserved
                               decisions return to the appropriate authority.


 Autonomy is multidimensional: an agent may have wide freedom to select a retrieval method,
 narrow freedom to commit money and no freedom to disclose private information. The same is true
 of humans working within organizations. Guidance can be distinguished from strict prohibitions;
 uncertainties and conflicts must be resolved rather than assumed away.

 Documentation, CRDs, permissions and policies are therefore more than development instructions.
 They can represent accumulated human decisions, made durable and applicable to future actions.
 Systems need traceability, revocable delegation, clear escalation, appropriate oversight and
 mechanisms to update those decisions as circumstances change.


    Higher-level decisions define the space of freedom for lower-level
    decisions, rather than requiring every lower-level decision to be
    repeated.

 The internet beyond applications
 The Internet on One Page model, provided by Loikkanen, describes the digital economy through
 fundamental activities, information flows, information types and revenue models. Its logic does not
 depend on particular applications. Liquid Software provides a possible technical means of serving
 these activities without fixing them to a permanent application or platform.




 Figure 1. The Internet on One Page. Source: Valto Loikkanen, diagram supplied in the source discussion. Cropped to the
 diagram boundary.

 Create, Control, Promote, Connect, Discover, Acquire and Consume remain meaningful regardless
 of who performs them or what software is used. They can be supported by agents, applications or
 people acting for individuals and entities. Information can carry promotional, public-domain, owned
 or private/personal value; the relevant rights and access conditions govern exchange.

 Licensing, advertising, transaction fees, commerce and subscriptions are likewise durable economic
 arrangements. An agent could discover licensable information, evaluate terms, obtain authorized
 access and utilize it for its principal without a dedicated marketplace UI. Intermediaries and
 platforms may still deliver trust, liquidity and coordination; their role need not be dictated solely by
 software constraints.

 The opportunity is to separate the enduring economic activity from its transient technological
 implementation. Trust, authority, information quality and human governance become increasingly
 important as software becomes easier to adapt.

 From thesis to practice
 The proposed concepts share a reversal: preserve what has enduring value, make boundaries
 explicit and allow implementations and representations to evolve around actual needs. They
 operate at different levels and should remain independently understandable and adoptable. The
 reference architecture is a way of combining them, not the only way to implement them.

 A practical transition pathway
   Establish foundations        Identify canonical sources, ownership, permissions, terminology, CRDs and
                                guidance for unspecified cases.

   Introduce adaptive access    Support context-driven human and machine requests; separate response
                                contracts from fulfillment.

   Preserve richer evidence     Retain appropriate source information with provenance; distinguish canonical
                                and derived representations.

   Observe actual demand        Instrument usage with privacy-preserving monitoring and identify repetitive
                                needs.

   Optimize under               Create and retire validated software and retrieval structures; benchmark
   governance                   against agentic and hybrid paths.

   Increase decision leverage   Define delegated freedom, escalation and reserved decisions; adjust
                                authority through deliberate review.



 Open research and validation questions
 How can agents converge on consistent semantics without rigidly predetermining every request?
 How should evolving contracts be discovered, negotiated and versioned? How can equivalence
 between agentic and deterministic responses be tested, including uncertainty and provenance?
 What retention choices best balance future information value, rights, environmental cost and
 storage economics? When does the cost of autonomous optimization outweigh its benefit? How
 can decision boundaries be made inspectable and enforceable across independently operated
 systems?

 These questions define a research and implementation agenda. The architecture should be tested
 through concrete workloads, measuring accuracy, permission compliance, latency, cost, portability
 and the ability to retire implementations without loss of continuity.


    Define what must remain consistent. Allow everything else to adapt
    intelligently.

 Conclusion and source note
 The enduring elements of the digital world are not necessarily the applications through which we
 currently experience it. They are the individuals and entities being served, the information and
 knowledge they retain, the intentions and relationships they establish, the economic activities they
 undertake and the decisions that govern their conduct.

 When intelligence makes information accessible without extensive prior specification, storage can
 preserve future value, interfaces can serve immediate context and software can evolve in response
 to actual demand. The resulting world need not be less coherent or less human-controlled.
 Coherence comes from shared semantics, documentation, capabilities and enforceable boundaries;
 sovereignty comes from authority and degrees of decision freedom.

 This is a proposed change in the relationship between information, intelligence and software. Its
 realization depends on reliable implementations, evidence-preserving practices, interoperability,
 sound governance and the deliberate protection of rights.


 Information provides continuity.
 Human decisions define the boundaries.
 Intelligence fulfills needs.
 Software adapts.
