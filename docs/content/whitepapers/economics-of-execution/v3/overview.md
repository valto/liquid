## What should exist?

Faster code generation changes the cost of producing software. The paper asks an additional question: when does the underlying need require a dedicated implementation at all?

A capability can combine general-purpose reasoning, existing software, skills, exact functions, declared rules, models and APIs. The economically meaningful unit can be a constituent of a larger activity. Selecting one realization does not require rebuilding the entire application or using one model for every step.

The objective is expected cumulative net value over time within requirements for accepted outcomes, quality, reliability, privacy, safety and authority. Runtime price alone is sufficient only when outcomes, timing and relevant risks are comparable. Stable software, existing SaaS, manual fulfillment and retaining the current system remain legitimate alternatives.

## What v3 makes more explicit

**Serve, observe and invest selectively.** Where an acceptable initial path exists, serving a need can reveal demand and exceptions before major construction. Options to defer, expand, change or abandon investment can have value. Waiting has costs too: urgent capacity and hard requirements may justify upfront specialization. Reward business outcomes and avoided waste rather than application or agent counts.

**Compare sequences.** A strategy may serve now with an agent, build a validated function in parallel, then specialize if future demand justifies it. Each stage and transition must be feasible. Early fulfillment does not prove that a later build is worthwhile.

**Distinguish constraints.** Stable safety, privacy and authority controls may be necessary. Incidental workflow limitations can be reconsidered. Flexibility has economic value when future changes are plausible and consequential; architectural elegance alone is not a valuation method.

**Amortize at the actual boundary.** Market demand can repay a vendor's shared product. A private implementation depends on attributable entity use, including reuse across activities. Buying or renting existing tools can still be the best choice.

**Count the cost of adaptability.** Interfaces, dependencies, validation, change reviews and the optimizer itself consume resources. Retain stability when change brings no material benefit. Retirement must preserve commitments and source evidence or explicitly revise the commitment through appropriate authority.

## Checked illustrative calculations

These are supplied assumptions, not market prices, deployment results or forecasts. Arithmetic checks do not validate the economic model.

| Example | Assumptions | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Break-even, p. 10 | Fixed software €1,200; agent €2.50/use; software €0.10/use | Equal cost at 500 future executions. At 100 uses: €250 versus €1,210; at 1,000: €2,500 versus €1,300. |
| Six-month contribution, p. 15 | Both build a €12,000 module. Months 4–6 net €4,500/month; the agent path additionally nets €2,500/month in months 1–3 | Software-first €1,500; agent-first then module €9,000; difference €7,500. |
| Moving thresholds, p. 17 | Fixed €1,200 and software €0.10/use; agent €2.50, €1.30 or €0.50 | Thresholds 500, 1,000 and 3,000. Software-production costs can change too. |
| Retrieval, p. 23 | Preprocessing/upkeep €1,300, then €0.01/retrieval; on-demand €0.20/retrieval | At 1,000: €1,310 versus €200. At 10,000: €1,400 versus €2,000. Continuous crossover 6,842.105…; the first whole count favoring preprocessing is **6,843**. |
| Reused function, p. 28 | Fixed €900; agent €0.40/use; function €0.02/use | At 6,000: €2,400 versus €1,020, saving €1,380. At 1,000: €400 versus €920; a new build does not repay itself here. |

The source rounds the retrieval crossover to approximately 6,842. The whole-count note is editorial; original wording remains intact. Retrieval events need not be unique documents: the 10,000-document archive size is distinct from demand. Basic indexing and access controls are assumed equally available to both paths.

Past usage is sunk; thresholds concern remaining demand and future incremental investment. An already-built function needs a comparison of avoidable future costs rather than another construction charge. Released staff capacity is not automatically cash savings: identify conversion into avoided hiring, useful throughput or another accepted benefit.

Positive discounting reduces the present value of a fixed future cash flow; earlier receipt can improve value relative to later receipt. It does not make the same early cash flows worth more than their undiscounted sum.

## Maturity means informed selection

Page 26 distinguishes **understanding maturity**, **experiential maturity** and **implementation maturity**. Broader awareness can reveal more options while leaving a conventional deterministic service as the safest or most valuable choice.

V3 defines **maturity bias** as limitations to perceived alternatives arising from current understanding and experience. This expands the v2-derived explanation's warning against judging decision quality by position on a presumed implementation progression. The formulations are distinct and the revision is attributed explicitly.

The spectrum from traditional software through AI-enabled, agentic, agent-first and AI-native operations, plus unknown further paradigms, is conceptual. It is not a validated personal assessment or an instruction to maximize agents. Practical exposure can inform selection; an AI-written application or a permanently packaged agent does not prove wider alternatives were considered.
