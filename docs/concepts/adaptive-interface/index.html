The Adaptive Interface

Presentation and interaction respond to what is needed, by whom, where and why; how is a contextual result.

Conceptual source: Valto Loikkanen · AI-assisted synthesis and research

Read source text ↗

Definition. Presentation and interaction respond to what is needed, by whom, where and why; how is a contextual result.

Information exists independently of one screen, document, dashboard or request format. The interface is selected or generated around the recipient, purpose, environment and nature of the information.

A human may use speech, a diagram or text; an agent may use natural language or a structured request. These can express related needs while preserving their distinct requirements for accessibility, precision and authority.

Languages and formats

Begin with how participants express needs and carry information. Shared terminology and commitments guide interpretation. A schema can be generated, inferred or negotiated where useful; recognizable syntax alone does not establish agreed meaning.

Preserve the relationship

Changing presentation does not confer new permission or alter the underlying commitment. Test comprehension, task outcomes, accessibility, uncertainty and the ability to move between representations without losing relevant evidence.
