Liquid Software

Definition. Implementations emerge, adapt, stabilize and retire around actual needs, within durable semantics and human-defined boundaries.

Software fluidity concerns the implementation of a capability. Meaning, accepted commitments, information ownership and authority must remain understandable as that implementation changes.

UI on the fly and backend on the fly describe creation or adaptation in response to context. Liquid Software extends the principle to integrations, retrieval and execution. A deterministic implementation can remain the best choice for years; fluidity includes choosing stability.

A reversible lifecycle

A novel need may be fulfilled intelligently. Recurring demand can justify a validated query, cache, service or other optimization. Changed needs may require adaptation. Declining demand may justify retiring that realization while preserving the promised capability or deliberately revising its commitment.

Direct execution, reusable skills, deterministic functions, modules and dedicated software form a reversible continuum, not a mandatory maturity progression. Different operations can occupy different points at the same time. Modularity supports that choice when its coordination, interface and assurance costs are justified.

Materialization fixes assumptions about workflows, data, interaction and rules. Evaluate the flexibility it removes along with the performance it gains. Independent functions and permitted alternative paths can keep exceptions workable without silently revising the capability's commitments.

Examine the tradeoff

Measure quality, latency, cost, reliability, reversibility and the effort needed to preserve meaning. Changing software is useful when it improves fulfillment, not merely because generation is available.

Use cumulative net value, including time to value and the full lifecycle, to compare feasible realizations. Least-cost selection is sufficient only when outcomes, timing and relevant risks are comparable. Changed needs can justify evolving or dissolving an implementation while retaining source continuity.

Evolve without forcing continuous change

Sensing, learning, reviewing, releasing and adopting can proceed at different rates. A system can learn continuously while keeping a proven interface or process stable. Stability-Aware Evolution compares future outcomes together with disruption and transition costs.

Parallel Realizations allows a candidate to be tested, released to a bounded context or retained alongside an existing version. Release, adoption and retirement are separate decisions. Multiple active versions can be justified by different tasks and preferences, subject to compatibility and support costs.
